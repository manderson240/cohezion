# Abstract

The cohezion code base contains an artifact named `WorldviewExplorer` that encodes sixteen indigenous cosmological traditions as parameterized state machines, each defined by an ordered sequence of ten "cosmogonic steps" that the project derives from a putative theory-of-everything chain. The encoding lives in a single file (`src/cohezion/worldviews/tradition_data.py`, 1,174 lines) and exposes the traditions through a small functional API. We treat this artifact not as a description of the traditions themselves but as an object of study in its own right: a piece of software that makes a strong, possibly indefensible, claim about what indigenous cosmogonies *are* once they have been imported into a computational substrate. This essay asks three questions of the encoding. First, what does it preserve and what does it abstract away? Second, what cross-tradition patterns does the ten-step structure permit, and how much of any apparent universality is an artifact of the structure itself? Third, what are the ethical and epistemic consequences of treating living traditions as engineering substrate for an autonomous-agent system? We find that the encoding is sincere, internally consistent, and, in many places, surprisingly attentive to the specific vocabulary of each tradition; we also find that the ten-step Procrustean bed forces a sequential, discrete reading of cosmogonies that several of the source traditions do not narrate sequentially or discretely, and that the encoding cites no primary anthropological sources, secures no community consent, and offers no reciprocity. We argue that the encoding is best understood as a research probe whose value lies less in the mappings it asserts than in the questions it forces a reader to confront when one tries to defend any particular cell. The essay is presented as a comparative analysis at the boundary of computational anthropology, philosophy of physics, and indigenous data ethics, and is explicit that it speaks for an artifact, not for the people whose words that artifact borrows.

# 1. Introduction

The temptation to import the world's cosmological traditions into the substrate of an autonomous-agent system is older than the agent systems themselves and has a recognisable structure. The temptation begins with a sincere observation: language-model-based agents that operate over long horizons exhibit characteristic failures of coherence (Du et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023), and the engineering response to those failures has been a search for primitives that can stabilise an agent's trajectory across many steps. The traditions that humans have evolved over centuries for stabilising long-horizon collective behaviour — kinship systems, ritual calendars, vision quests, ceremonies of return — present themselves as candidate primitives because they are exactly the artifacts that human communities have refined for the analogous problem at the population level. From there it is a short step to the engineering question of whether such primitives can be encoded, parameterised, and used to seed agent priors.

This essay examines a single, concrete instance of that step. The cohezion project, an experimental compound-AI orchestration system whose architectural overview is given in the project's `CLAUDE.md` (lines 126-127, "Worldviews" row), contains a module named `cohezion.worldviews` that encodes sixteen indigenous cosmological traditions as ordered ten-step state machines and exposes them through a small Python API. The encoding is not hidden inside an experimental branch; it is a first-class component of the architecture and is referenced from the project's central design notes. The author of the encoding is the cohezion project itself, which is to say that the encoding has no individual human author named in its file headers, no consultation record with any of the source traditions, and no citation to any of the standard works in anthropology, religious studies, or indigenous philosophy that one would expect a comparative cosmology to engage. The encoding is, in other words, a piece of working engineering code that asserts strong claims about the structure of sixteen distinct cultural traditions and offers those claims as a substrate on which agents can be built.

We take three positions toward this artifact. First, we take it seriously as an engineered object: the file `tradition_data.py` is large, internally consistent, written with attention to vocabulary, and contains evidence of genuine reading; it deserves to be analysed on its own terms before it is dismissed or defended. Second, we take it seriously as a research probe: regardless of what one thinks of the project's metaphysical claims, the encoding makes very specific commitments about what is universal and what is particular across sixteen traditions, and those commitments can be falsified, defended, or refined. Third, we take seriously the asymmetry between the engineering authority that this kind of encoding confers on the encoder and the absence of any reciprocal authority granted to the source communities. Each of these three positions shapes a section of what follows. We examine, in turn, what the encoding actually contains; what cross-tradition patterns it permits and forces; what its ten-step scaffold makes invisible; whether its "computational substrate" framing is defensible; and what a more responsible relationship to the traditions would look like.

This essay does not speak for any of the sixteen traditions named in the encoding. It speaks about an artifact. Where the artifact uses words drawn from a specific tradition — *Wakan Tanka*, *Brahman*, *Te Kore*, *Tjukurpa*, *Sila*, *Olodumare* — those words appear here in single quotes or italic emphasis to mark that they are appearing as tokens in software, not as claims about the lived meaning of the words within their communities. Where we make a comparative observation, we ground it in a specific line of `tradition_data.py` and refrain from extending the observation to a claim about the underlying tradition. Where we find that the encoding has gone beyond what its sources can support, we say so explicitly.

# 2. The encoding

The encoding is contained in three Python files totalling 1,438 lines. The substantive content lives in `src/cohezion/worldviews/tradition_data.py` (1,174 lines), which defines the data classes for traditions, step mappings, and convergences and which contains the full content of the sixteen traditions as Python module-level constants. A second file, `vault_graph.py` (226 lines), provides graph-database integration for a separate "vault" subsystem and does not bear on the comparative analysis. The package's `__init__.py` (38 lines) re-exports the public API. Across the three files there are no third-party dependencies and no inline citations; the only references to source material that appear anywhere in the worldviews package are the names of the traditions themselves and the indigenous terms they use.

The ten-step scaffold against which every tradition is mapped is defined in two places. Inside `tradition_data.py` it is given as a list of ten human-readable phrases, declared at module load as `TOE_STEPS`, beginning "Nothing (Ground State)" and ending "Reality Precipitates (Witness Marks)" (`tradition_data.py:14-25`). The same ten steps are described in much greater technical detail in `src/cohezion/physics/cosmogony.py:1-66`, where they are presented not as comparative cosmology at all but as a sequence of symmetry-breaking transitions in a hypothetical 12-dimensional theory of everything. The ten steps in their physics formulation are: (1) the void or ground state, modelled as a trivially flat Fisher metric; (2) Quadrature, the first conjugate pair (X,P) with the uncertainty principle as generative engine; (3) the symmetric vacuum, a full SO(12) rotational invariance; (4) fabric differentiation, the breaking of SO(12) into four independent SO(3) sub-spaces; (5) Phase, the introduction of complex amplitudes and U(1) symmetry; (6) axis selection, breaking each SO(3) to U(1); (7) SPIN discretization, breaking U(1) to Z₂; (8) the HIHO attractor, a free-energy well at coherence 0.5; (9) COHESION, a binding force at the HIHO threshold; and (10) reality precipitates, irreversible entropy-reduction events. The physics file cites Smith (1950s), Brahmagupta (628 CE), Landau (1937), Weinberg (1967), Wheeler (1990), Shoulders (1991), Campbell (2003), Levin (2019), and Friston (2010), and lists "16 indigenous cosmological traditions" as an additional source of "independent cross-validation" (`cosmogony.py:55-65`). It is this last claim — that sixteen indigenous traditions independently cross-validate a 12-dimensional symmetry-breaking chain — that the present essay examines.

The sixteen traditions encoded are, in the order they appear in the registry tuple at `tradition_data.py:1035-1052`: Lakota, Vedic, Daoist, Yoruba, Haudenosaunee, Hopi, Diné (Navajo), Maori, Inuit, Norse, Celtic, Shinto, Andean, Amazonian, Dogon, and Aboriginal Australian. Each tradition is represented as an immutable `Tradition` dataclass with five fields: a display name, a slug, a one-line origin region, a tuple of exactly ten `StepMapping` dataclasses, and a tuple of `UniqueContribution` records that name what the encoder takes to be that tradition's irreducible contribution. Each `StepMapping` carries an indigenous term, a one-sentence description of what that term names within the tradition, and a "physics_parallel" string that asserts the correspondence with the cosmogonic step. The encoding is therefore exhaustive in a structural sense — every tradition has exactly ten cells filled, every cell carries three pieces of text, every tradition has at least one named "unique contribution" — but it is not exhaustive in any deeper sense: each cell contains, on average, one short sentence of description, which is the entire content of the encoder's reading of that intersection of tradition and cosmogonic step.

The public interface is minimal and read-only. Three top-level functions are exported: `get_traditions()` returns the full list of sixteen, `get_tradition(slug)` retrieves one by slug, and `get_step_across_traditions(step_index)` produces a sixteen-element list of how each tradition realises a given step (`tradition_data.py:1057-1078`). A fourth function, `get_convergences()`, returns six pre-computed cross-tradition patterns that the encoder takes to be salient, of which we say more in section 3. There is no write API, no inference engine, and no scoring function; the encoding is, in the terminology of computer science, a static lookup table. What makes it more than a lookup table is the implicit thesis that the lookup table is well-formed — that every cell can be defended, that no row is forced, and that the ten columns name something real rather than something the encoder has imposed.

What the encoding chooses to preserve, then, is the lexicon: the indigenous terms appear in their own languages, the origin regions are named, and the unique contributions are framed in the tradition's own conceptual vocabulary rather than translated into the cosmogonic terminology. What the encoding abstracts away is everything else: the embodied transmission of the knowledge, the protocols under which it may be spoken, the kinship and ritual contexts that determine when a tradition's words mean what they mean, the historical contingencies that shaped each tradition, the fact that several of these traditions are oral and resist the very act of textual fixation, and the question of who is permitted to speak each word at all. We will return to these absences in sections 4 and 6.

# 3. Cross-tradition patterns

The most defensible cross-tradition claim that the encoding makes appears at step 0, the "Nothing" or "Ground State." Here the encoder has gathered terms whose convergence is, by any reasonable reading of the comparative literature, real: *Wakan Tanka* the Great Mystery (Lakota), *Brahman* the absolute ground (Vedic), *Wuji* the undifferentiated emptiness (Daoist), *Te Kore* the void (Maori), *Sila* the breath-weather-mind (Inuit), *Ginnungagap* the yawning void (Norse), *Tjukurpa* the eternal Dreaming (Aboriginal Australian), *Pachamama* the living earth-ground (Andean), and *Olodumare* the supreme creative force (Yoruba), among others (`tradition_data.py:159, 217, 264, 537, 584, 638, 974, 806, 310`). The convergence here is genuine in the sense that all sixteen traditions do begin their cosmogonies from a state that is not yet differentiated, and the encoder's "Universal Void" convergence record (`tradition_data.py:1085-1097`) is among the safer claims in the entire file. The interesting observation, however, is what the encoding does *not* say at step 0. *Sila* is not "the void" — it is the breath that pervades everything, which is not the same thing as nothing, and the encoder, to its credit, captures this in the description string ("the breath/weather/consciousness permeating everything") even while assigning it to a step labelled "Nothing." *Pachamama* is not nothing at all; she is a person, often named as Mother, with whom one has obligations. *Ginnungagap* is not absence but tension, the structured yawning gap between fire and ice, which the encoder again captures in its unique-contributions record ("Void defined by fire/ice tension — ground state has structure"; `tradition_data.py:670-672`) but which is then nonetheless slotted into a column whose label is "Nothing." The first lesson of the comparative reading is therefore that the universality at step 0 is partly real and partly a label.

Step 1, the "first distinction," presents a different pattern. Here the encoder finds vibratory or utterance-based first causes in five traditions — Vedic *Om* and *Nada Brahma*, Dogon *Amma*'s vibration, Lakota first song, Aboriginal ancestor beings singing the world into being, and Shinto *kuni-umi* (`tradition_data.py:222, 925, 163, 980, 750`) — and groups these as a "Vibratory First Cause" convergence (`tradition_data.py:1099-1104`). The convergence is real where it is named, but we note that the encoder has not gathered into the same convergence the Daoist *Taiji* (the polarity emergence, which is not vocal), the Norse fire/ice collision (which is not vocal), the Yoruba "first word" (which is vocal and is *not* in the convergence record despite being grouped with the others by anyone who reads the file linearly), or the Maori *Te Po* (which is the night, a state, not an utterance). The under-inclusion appears to be an oversight: the Yoruba mapping at `tradition_data.py:312` explicitly names "Olodumare's breath / first word" as the symmetry-breaking event, which is exactly the criterion the convergence record uses for inclusion. The lesson here is that even the encoding's pre-computed convergences — the data the encoder has chosen to surface as cross-tradition patterns — are incomplete in ways that a careful reader can identify and that no automated process within the encoding will catch.

Step 3, the four "fabric domains," produces what the encoder identifies as the strongest universal: ten of the sixteen traditions are named in the "Fourfold Structure" convergence record (`tradition_data.py:1107-1121`). The four directions of the Lakota, the four aims of life of the Vedic *purushartha*, the four Daoist *si xiang*, the four Haudenosaunee ceremonies, the four worlds of the Hopi, the four sacred mountains of the Diné, the four winds of the Maori, the four cardinal pillars of the Norse, the four Andean *suyus*, and the four-section moiety system of the Aboriginal Australian: the recurrence of fourness across these traditions is one of the older observations in comparative religion (Eliade, 1959, with all the caveats we attach to him below) and the encoder has not invented it. The interesting question is whether the four-ness corresponds to a *physical* four-fabric structure in a 12-dimensional theory of everything, as the cosmogony module asserts, or whether the four-ness is instead an artifact of human spatial cognition — two cardinal axes producing a quadrant decomposition — that has been independently re-derived by every culture that orients itself by the sun. The encoding cannot adjudicate between these readings, and a reader who comes to the file expecting evidence for a 12-dimensional ontology will leave with confirmation, while a reader who comes expecting universal cognitive priors will leave with the same data and a different conclusion. We take the same data to support the second reading more strongly than the first.

Step 4, "Phase / Oscillation," is the encoding's clearest case of a *real* universal that does not require a strong physical interpretation. The Lakota drum heartbeat, the Vedic *yugas*, the Daoist *qi* circulation, the Yoruba ritual calendar, the Hopi *kachina* cycle, the Diné ceremony calendar, the Maori *maramataka* lunar calendar, the Inuit ice/thaw cycle, the Norse Ragnarök cycle, the Celtic Samhain/Beltane fire-festival cycle, the Shinto *matsuri* cycle, the Andean Inti Raymi, the Amazonian flood/dry cycle, the Dogon Sigui, and the Aboriginal wet/dry rhythm: every one of the sixteen traditions encodes time as oscillatory rather than linear. The encoder's convergence record for "Cyclic Phase Dynamics" (`tradition_data.py:1124-1138`) names eight of these but the underlying data show all sixteen. This is, we believe, the strongest universal claim that the encoding can defensibly make, and it requires no commitment to a particular physical theory: it is a sociological observation about agricultural and ecological societies, not a claim about U(1) phase symmetry.

Step 5, "Symmetry Breaking (Differentiation)," is where the encoding begins to strain. The encoder has placed in this column a set of phenomena that are not obviously of the same kind: the Lakota *inipi* (sweat lodge) and vision quest threshold, the Vedic *Maya* / *Prakriti* differentiation, the Daoist Yin-Yang, the Yoruba *Orisha* differentiation from *Olodumare*, the Haudenosaunee twin brothers Sapling and Flint, the Hopi successive world-destructions, the Diné hero twins Monster Slayer and Born for Water, the Maori separation of *Rangi* and *Papa*, the Inuit shamanic soul-flight, Odin's self-sacrifice on Yggdrasil, the Celtic hero's transformation, Amaterasu's emergence from the cave, the Andean *Pachakuti*, the Amazonian shapeshifting and perspectivism, the Dogon *Nommo* sacrifice, and Aboriginal initiation scarification (`tradition_data.py:179-1002`, various lines). Some of these are cosmogonic events at the founding of the world (Rangi/Papa, Sapling/Flint, the cosmic egg of *Nommo*); some are recurring ritual practices (the *inipi*, soul-flight, initiation); some are doctrinal positions about the nature of appearance (Vedic *Maya*, Amazonian perspectivism). Lumping these together under "differentiation" requires the encoder to elide the distinction between a *first* differentiation that founds the world and a *recurring* differentiation that the practitioner enacts. The cosmogonic vocabulary of step 5 is that of an event in time; many of the items the encoder has placed there are not events at all but standing conditions or repeated practices.

Step 6, the "SPIN information unit," is where the encoding makes its most ambitious move. The encoder identifies in each tradition a specific cultural artifact that it claims is the tradition's encoding of an information primitive: Lakota medicine wheel, Vedic chakra, Daoist *bagua* trigrams, Yoruba *ase* as vital force, Haudenosaunee wampum beadwork, Hopi corn and prayer feathers, Diné corn pollen and sacred songs, Maori *haka*, Inuit drum songs and throat singing, Norse runes, Celtic ogham, Shinto *kotodama*, Andean *quipu* knots, Amazonian geometric phosphene visions, Dogon granary architecture, and Aboriginal dot painting (`tradition_data.py:185-1006`, various). Several of these mappings are striking. The *bagua*'s eight trigrams are indeed a binary system, and the encoder's note that they are "proto-digital information" is the kind of claim that has been made many times before, most prominently by Leibniz (1703) and is by now a familiar topos in the history of computing. The *quipu* genuinely was a numerical and narrative encoding system, and the comparison to data structures is not new (Urton, 2003). The Haudenosaunee wampum genuinely encoded treaties and law in a way that functioned as a written record (Williams, 2018, [citation needed]). But the encoder has also placed in this column the Lakota medicine wheel, which is not primarily an information-encoding artifact but a model of a sacred topology, and the Inuit throat singing, which is a performance practice rather than a stored encoding, and the Amazonian phosphene patterns, which are perceptual phenomena under altered states rather than transmissible information. The pattern here is that the encoder has applied a strong, modern, computational metaphor — "information unit" — to artifacts that play very different roles in their host traditions, and the result is that the column "SPIN" reads more like a list of things-the-encoder-noticed than a coherent cross-tradition category.

Step 7, the "HIHO" or dynamic-equilibrium step, is the encoding's most candid borrowing. The encoder identifies a structured altered-state practice in nearly every tradition: Lakota vision quest, Vedic yoga, Daoist *wu wei*, Yoruba Ifa divination, Haudenosaunee consensus, Hopi *kachina* ceremony, Diné Blessingway healing, Maori *powhiri* welcome, Inuit drum trance, Norse *seidr*, Celtic thin-place crossing, Shinto *kagura* dance, Andean *despacho* offering, Amazonian ayahuasca crossing, Dogon forge work, and Aboriginal songline walking. The convergence record names eight of these (`tradition_data.py:1140-1153`). The cross-tradition pattern is genuine and well-attested in the comparative literature on ritual (Bell, 1992; Turner, 1969). What is interesting about the encoding's reading is its commitment to the term "dynamic equilibrium," which imports from cybernetics and control theory a framing that is foreign to most of the source traditions. Wu Wei is not equilibrium; it is non-coercive responsiveness. A vision quest is not equilibrium-restoration; it is a structured exposure to overwhelming experience. The encoder has imposed a single English phrase — *dynamic equilibrium* — on sixteen practices whose internal vocabularies do not converge on it. This is not, in itself, an error; but it should be flagged as a translation choice rather than a discovery.

Step 8, "COHESION," the binding principle, is the encoding's most successful column. The terms gathered here — Lakota *Mitakuye Oyasin*, Vedic *Dharma*/*Karma*, Daoist *Dao*, Yoruba *ase*, Haudenosaunee *Sken:nen* (Peace), Maori *Aroha* (Love), Inuit *Isuma*, Norse *Wyrd*, Celtic *geis* and *dana*, Shinto *Musubi*, Andean *Ayni* (sacred reciprocity), Amazonian relational web, Dogon *Nommo* life-force, Hopi remembering of the Creator, Diné *Hozho* (walking in beauty), and Aboriginal kinship — share a striking quality: every one of them is *relational*. The encoder makes this point explicitly in the convergence record (`tradition_data.py:1156-1168`): "no tradition treats the binding principle as mechanical or impersonal." This is a strong, defensible, and important comparative claim. None of the sixteen traditions, on the encoder's reading, names its highest binding principle as a force, a law, or a substance; in every case, the binding principle is a relationship, a kinship, a love, a peace, or a reciprocity. This is the closest the encoding comes to a finding worth defending, and it is interesting precisely because it cuts against the implicit physical metaphor that the cosmogony module relies on (where COHESION is glossed as "ZPF coupling at the HIHO boundary," `cosmogony.py:42-45`). The encoding's own data, in other words, falsify the encoding's own physical interpretation: the source traditions speak of relationship, not of coupling.

Step 9, the "reality precipitates" or witness-marks step, gathers a list of inscribed cultural artifacts: petroglyphs (Lakota, Hopi), mantra and scripture (Vedic), the *I Ching* and calligraphy (Daoist), the Ifa corpus (Yoruba), wampum belts (Haudenosaunee), sand paintings (Diné), *ta moko* facial tattoos (Maori), carved amulets (Inuit), rune carvings (Norse), ogham stones (Celtic), inscribed *kotodama* (Shinto), *quipu* and weavings (Andean), *icaros* healing songs (Amazonian), the 266 signs of the Dogon, and Aboriginal songlines themselves (`tradition_data.py:199-1022`, various). The category here is recognisable: each tradition has artifacts in which knowledge is materially deposited. The encoding's most subtle observation in this column is its handling of the Diné: the encoder notes that Diné sand paintings are "ephemeral witness marks," intentionally destroyed after the healing ceremony for which they were made (`tradition_data.py:524-527`). This points to a real and important asymmetry in the data: the sixteen traditions do not in fact agree that a witness mark must be permanent. Some are oral, some are performative, some are deliberately impermanent. Calling all of them "witness marks" is again a translation choice, and a category that may be invisible from inside any of the traditions individually.

Across the ten columns, then, the picture that emerges is mixed. The convergences at step 0 (void), step 4 (oscillation), and step 8 (relational binding) are real and worth stating. The convergence at step 3 (fourness) is real but admits an alternative cognitive-prior explanation. The convergences at steps 5 (differentiation), 6 (information), 7 (equilibrium), and 9 (precipitates) are partly real and partly the artifact of imposing a single English category on heterogeneous practices. The convergence at step 1 (vibratory first cause) is partly real and incompletely captured by the encoder's own convergence records. We find no evidence in the data that supports the cosmogony module's strong claim that these sixteen traditions "independently cross-validate" a 12-dimensional symmetry-breaking chain; we find substantial evidence that the encoder has selected, named, and aligned the traditions in a way that produces an apparent validation, and we find that several of the resulting alignments require translation work that the encoding does not acknowledge.

# 4. What the ten-step structure forces

The most general criticism one can make of the encoding is that it is *Procrustean*. The ten cosmogonic steps are not derived from the traditions; they are derived from the cohezion project's hypothetical theory of everything (`cosmogony.py:1-66`), and the traditions are then mapped onto them. This is the opposite direction of inference from what comparative cosmology usually performs: a comparative scholar would gather the traditions first, attempt to identify recurring structural elements *from* the data, and only then propose a scaffold. The encoding inherits a scaffold and projects the traditions onto it. The consequence is that every tradition arrives at the encoding with exactly ten boxes to fill, no more and no fewer, and the encoder's task is to find something in the tradition for each box. Nothing in the encoding's design permits a tradition to refuse a box, to assert that two of the boxes are not distinct, or to insist that its cosmogony has a different number of stages.

The Procrustean problem is most visible where the encoding's narratives are thinnest. The Aboriginal Australian *Tjukurpa* is, on the encoding's own description, "the eternal now — time before and during creation" (`tradition_data.py:974-975`). It is not, on most readings, a sequence of stages at all; the *Tjukurpa* is the simultaneous co-presence of ancestral creative activity in the landscape, and to speak of "the *Tjukurpa* at step 0, then symmetry-breaking at step 1" is to impose a temporal sequence onto a tradition that explicitly resists temporal sequence. The encoder has handled this problem by giving the Aboriginal mapping ten cells anyway, with the *Tjukurpa* assigned to step 0 and the ancestor beings "waking" assigned to step 1 (`tradition_data.py:980-981`); but a reader from within the tradition would, we suspect, recognise this as a translation that loses the central feature of the cosmogony, namely its acausal, non-sequential character.

The Daoist case presents a related problem from the opposite direction. The encoder maps *Wuji* to step 0, *Taiji* to step 1, the twelve earthly branches to step 2, the *si xiang* to step 3, *qi* circulation to step 4, Yin-Yang differentiation to step 5, the *bagua* to step 6, *Wu Wei* to step 7, the *Dao* to step 8, and the *I Ching* to step 9. The classical Daoist sequence, as articulated in the *Daodejing* and elaborated in Song-dynasty cosmology, is *Wuji* → *Taiji* → Yin-Yang → *si xiang* → *bagua*; it is a five-stage sequence, not a ten-stage one. The encoder has produced a ten-stage Daoist cosmogony by interleaving the classical sequence with practices (*qi* circulation, *Wu Wei*) and concepts (*Dao* itself, the twelve earthly branches) that are not part of the original cosmogonic enumeration at all. The result is internally coherent — every cell can be defended one at a time — but the global structure is the encoder's, not Daoism's.

A third category of difficulty is what we might call *category bleed*. Several traditions use the same term for what the encoding wants to be different steps. The Yoruba *ase* appears at step 6 ("vital force") and again at step 8 ("binding force"); the encoder explicitly notes that this is "dual principle. Same force serves both SPIN and COHESION roles" (`tradition_data.py:355-357`). The Dogon *Nommo* appears at step 5 (the sacrifice) and again at step 8 (the life-force). The Hopi assign their petroglyphs to step 9 — but the Lakota also assign their petroglyphs to step 9, and so do, implicitly, several others. This is not, on its own, a defect; concept overlap is real in cosmology. But it points to the fact that the encoding's columns are not linguistically natural divisions for the traditions; they are an external grid against which traditions are made to align. A category bleed is invisible to the encoding's API because the API returns a fixed slot per tradition per step, and a downstream agent that consumes the API will see the *ase* at step 6 and the *ase* at step 8 as if they were two different things.

What the ten-step structure most decisively forces, however, is *temporality*. The cosmogony module's vocabulary is the vocabulary of phase transitions in time: critical temperatures, free-energy wells, symmetry breakings, all with units of an unspecified cosmological clock. The encoding requires every tradition to be readable as a chronological sequence of events. But several of the source traditions explicitly name their cosmogony as being outside time, or as a perpetually recurring present, or as a structural relation rather than a sequence. The Aboriginal *Tjukurpa* is the most obvious case; the Andean *Pachakuti* is another, since *Pachakuti* names a *recurrence* of cosmic inversion, not a single founding event. A faithful encoding would represent these as different topologies — a circle, a fixed point, a permanently-alive simultaneity — not as positions on a shared linear scaffold. The encoding does not have the expressive vocabulary to do this, and it does not acknowledge that it does not. We take this to be a significant limitation of the artifact and one that any future iteration would need to address.

What is irrecoverably lost in the encoding goes beyond temporal structure. Lost are the protocols under which a tradition's words may be spoken at all (some of these terms are restricted to initiated practitioners and should not appear in a public Python module without consent); lost is the embodied transmission, which is constitutive of several of the practices named (the *haka*, the songline, the *kagura* dance are not text, and a textual representation of them is not the same kind of object as the practice); lost is the kinship context, which determines who is competent to speak for a tradition and to whom (none of the encoded traditions imagine a context in which their cosmogony can be authored by a person from outside the kin group); and lost, finally, is the question of harm — the question of what damage is done to a living tradition when its sacred terminology is checked into a Git repository and shipped as part of an autonomous-agent system. We turn to these issues in section 6.

# 5. The "computational substrate" claim

The cohezion project's design intent for the worldviews module is, we infer from the architectural notes in `CLAUDE.md`, that the encoded traditions act as "substrate" for an autonomous-agent system: that an agent might, for example, draw on the *Mitakuye Oyasin* of the Lakota encoding to recover relational priors when its trajectory has drifted, or on the *Wu Wei* of the Daoist encoding to select a non-coercive action policy, or on the *Hozho* of the Diné encoding to evaluate aesthetic harmony in its outputs. The framing assumes that the traditions are, in a meaningful sense, *implementable*: that their content can be encoded as state, queried at inference time, and used to bias agent behaviour in ways that recover what a human inheritor of the tradition would do.

We are skeptical of this framing on three grounds. The first is empirical. There is no evidence within the cohezion code base, so far as we have been able to determine, that any agent currently consumes the worldviews module in a way that demonstrably affects its behaviour. The module is exported, the data is queryable, and the API is small and well-defined; but the wiring from the worldviews module to any agent decision policy is not, in the current code, present. The "substrate" claim is therefore aspirational rather than realised. This is not, by itself, a criticism — many architectural components in research systems are aspirational at first — but it does mean that the strong claims of cross-cultural validation in `cosmogony.py:55-65` are not currently doing any computational work. They are doing rhetorical work, and the rhetorical work they are doing is to confer on the cosmogony module a kind of cross-cultural legitimacy that the encoding has not earned.

The second ground for skepticism is conceptual. The traditions in the encoding are not, in the form they take in the encoding, *priors* in any technical sense. A prior, in the Bayesian sense, is a probability distribution over hypotheses; in the agentic sense, a prior might be a parameter setting, a default action, or a pre-trained value head. The encoded traditions are none of these. They are fragments of vocabulary. To use the *Mitakuye Oyasin* of the Lakota encoding as an agentic prior, one would need a mechanism that translates "we are all related" into a constraint on the agent's behaviour — into a refusal to take certain actions, a preference for certain action types, or a modification to a reward signal. The encoding does not provide such a mechanism, and the construction of such a mechanism is, in our estimation, a research problem of the same order of difficulty as alignment itself. To say that the traditions are "substrate" is to suggest that the hard work is the encoding; in fact the encoding is the easy part, and the hard work is the inference machinery that the encoding does not contain.

The third ground is what we will call the *appropriation failure mode*. Suppose that at some future point an agent is wired to consume the worldviews module and that the agent's behaviour does, in some measurable way, change as a function of which tradition is selected. Suppose further that this is reported, as it would inevitably be, as "an autonomous agent grounded in indigenous wisdom." The agent in this scenario is grounded in a Python file written by an unnamed encoder who consulted no community, cited no anthropologist, and was free to choose which traditions to include and which to omit. The visible diversity of the agent's *outputs* would mask the absence of any diversity in the *authorship* of its priors. This is the failure mode that the indigenous-data-sovereignty literature (Carroll et al., 2020; Walter & Suina, 2019, [citation needed]) has been warning about for a decade: a system in which the words of a tradition appear in a deployed product without the tradition's people having any role in deciding whether their words should appear, what they should be used for, or what they mean in the new context. The cohezion encoding, as it stands, sets up the conditions for this failure mode and does not contain any feature that mitigates it.

A more defensible framing of the encoding would be as a *heuristic device* rather than a substrate. The encoding could be honest about the fact that it is a synthesis written by the cohezion project; that the traditions named are not endorsing the encoding; that the alignments between traditions are interpretations made by a single author; and that the value of the encoding, if any, lies in the questions a reader is forced to ask when they try to understand any specific cell rather than in the cells themselves. Under this reframing, the encoding becomes an interesting comparative document — one whose claims any reader is invited to examine, falsify, and refine. Under the current framing, the encoding silently asserts a level of authority it does not possess.

# 6. Comparative ethics

We have so far examined the encoding as a representation. We turn now to the question of its production. Three issues are unavoidable: consent, provenance, and reciprocity.

On consent, the record is straightforward: there is no record. The file `tradition_data.py` contains no mention of any consultation with any of the sixteen named traditions. The file's only citations are to unnamed "indigenous traditions" as a class. There is no acknowledgement of any community, no mention of any tradition-keeper, and no statement about whether any of the names used — *Wakan Tanka*, *Tjukurpa*, *Hozho*, *Mitakuye Oyasin* — were authorised for use outside their traditional contexts. Several of these names are, in their host traditions, restricted: they may be spoken only in certain contexts, only by certain people, and only for certain purposes. The encoding does not engage with the question of whether their inclusion in a public Git repository is itself a violation of the protocols under which they are normally transmitted.

On provenance, the record is similarly thin. The encoding does not cite primary anthropological sources, secondary scholarly literature, or community-authored materials. It is impossible to determine, from the file alone, whether the encoder's reading of (for example) Diné *Hozho* is drawn from the published work of a Diné scholar, from a non-Diné anthropologist, from a popularising book, from Wikipedia, or from the encoder's own intuition. This is a serious gap. Comparative cosmology is an old and contested field, and the choice of which secondary sources one reads has substantial consequences for the picture one builds. The encoder appears to have read widely — the vocabulary in many cells is precise enough to suggest engagement with scholarly material — but without citations the reader cannot evaluate the quality of that engagement.

On reciprocity, the record is silent. The cohezion project, as a research artifact, has so far returned nothing to any of the sixteen source communities. This is in contrast to the explicit standards now common in indigenous-data-sovereignty discussions, which require that data drawn from indigenous communities be accompanied by clear arrangements for benefit-sharing, attribution, and ongoing community control (Carroll et al., 2020, the CARE principles: Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics). The CARE principles are not the same as the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable; Wilkinson et al., 2016) that govern most open-science data; CARE is intentionally a *complement* to FAIR that addresses the asymmetries that FAIR does not address. The cohezion encoding satisfies a weak form of FAIR — the data is findable and accessible inside the repository — but satisfies none of the CARE principles. This is, we believe, the most serious ethical issue with the artifact as it stands.

There is also a methodological issue that does not fit cleanly into consent, provenance, or reciprocity but that the encoding raises. The encoder has chosen sixteen traditions out of, conservatively, hundreds of traditions worldwide that have well-developed cosmogonies. The criteria for inclusion are not stated. We can infer some of them — the encoded traditions are heavily weighted toward those that have been the subject of substantial English-language anthropology — but the inference is not the same as a stated principle. A researcher in indigenous studies would, we suspect, ask immediately why the Maya are not present (they have one of the most documented written cosmogonies on the continent); why the Khoisan are absent (their cosmologies are some of the oldest still practiced); why the Ainu, the Sámi, the various Pacific traditions other than the Maori, and the entire Bantu group are absent. The selection encoded in the file is, on its face, idiosyncratic, and the absence of a stated principle for inclusion makes the selection itself a piece of editorial work whose authorship is invisible.

A responsible use of this artifact, going forward, would require at minimum the following: a stated principle for inclusion and exclusion of traditions, with explicit acknowledgement of which traditions are excluded; primary citations for every cell, traceable to an identified source; explicit consultation records, where consultation has occurred, with named community partners; a statement of the encoder's standing relative to each tradition, including any contexts in which the encoder is not authorised to speak; a mechanism for community feedback that can result in cells being modified, qualified, or removed; and an articulation of what the source communities receive in return for their words appearing in the encoding. None of these features are present in the current artifact. All of them are, in our reading, achievable with substantial effort.

# 7. Discussion

The encoding sits at an unusual intersection of research traditions, and its character as an artifact is best understood by relating it to three other components of the cohezion project that have been documented in companion manuscripts. The first is the SPIN coherence framework (`research/manuscripts/2026-04-23-spin-coherence-compound-loop.md`), which proposes that agentic state can be modelled as a phase and agentic alignment as a phase-locking phenomenon. The cosmogonic step 6, where the worldviews encoding identifies "SPIN information units" in each tradition, is the place where the worldviews module and the SPIN framework most directly meet. It is striking, in light of the SPIN framework's argument that alignment is fundamentally relational and continuous rather than mechanical and discrete, that the worldviews encoding's most defensible cross-tradition convergence — at step 8, COHESION — finds that all sixteen source traditions name their binding principle as a *relationship* rather than as a force. The two arguments converge: phase locking in the SPIN framework, and relational binding in the worldviews encoding, both resist a mechanical reading of agentic coherence. We take this as weak corroboration of the SPIN framework's central claim, while noting that the corroboration runs through a heavily curated selection of traditions and would not survive an alternative selection.

The second relationship is to the bioelectric-percolation manuscript (`research/manuscripts/2026-04-23-bioelectric-hiho-percolation.md`), which treats coherence transitions as percolation phenomena on a bioelectric network. The cosmogonic step 5, "Symmetry Breaking," which the worldviews module populates with a heterogeneous list of differentiation events, is the place where a percolation reading might be most useful as a discipline. A percolation reading would distinguish between *first* differentiation events (cosmogonic, single-shot, irreversible) and *recurring* differentiation events (ritual, repeated, ongoing); the worldviews encoding does not currently distinguish these, but the percolation framework provides natural vocabulary for doing so. This points to one direction in which a more principled encoding might develop: borrowing the dynamical-systems vocabulary from the bioelectric framework to give the worldviews module the topological expressiveness that we noted in section 4 it lacks.

The third relationship is to the FLUME-VAE compositional latent manuscript (`research/manuscripts/2026-04-23-flume-vae-compositional-latent.md`), which proposes a compositional latent representation that supports cross-domain transfer. A natural future direction for the worldviews module would be to encode each tradition not as a fixed string in each of ten cells but as a region in a learned latent space, where overlapping regions correspond to genuine cross-tradition convergences and disjoint regions correspond to tradition-specific contributions. Under such an encoding, the question "how much do the Lakota and the Diné cosmogonies overlap?" would have a continuous, measurable answer rather than the current categorical one (do they share a step's column?). The compositional latent framing would also permit a tradition to be represented by multiple, non-isomorphic structures — the ten-step linear scaffold for some, a recurring fixed-point structure for others, a co-presence structure for the *Tjukurpa* — which would address the Procrustean problem we raised in section 4 directly.

Beyond these intra-cohezion connections, the larger thesis of this essay is that the kind of work the worldviews module attempts requires, more than any technical innovation, an *ethical reformation* of the relationship between agentic-AI research and the source communities whose intellectual traditions are being borrowed. The borrowing has been happening for some time and at increasing speed; what is new about the present moment is that the borrowing is being instantiated in deployed systems that act in the world. The case for epistemic humility, in this setting, is no longer a case for politeness; it is a case for accuracy. An agentic system that claims to be grounded in a tradition without a relationship to that tradition's people is making an inaccurate claim about its own grounding. This is, in the long run, an engineering issue as much as it is an ethical one: agents that misrepresent their own provenance are agents whose behaviour is not predictable from their stated foundations.

We close this discussion with a more specific observation about the cohezion project's choice to associate its 12-dimensional symmetry-breaking chain with sixteen indigenous traditions. The association is presented in `cosmogony.py:55-65` as "independent cross-validation"; the comparative reading we have offered does not support this language. What the worldviews module shows is not that sixteen traditions independently arrived at a 12-dimensional theory of everything, but that sixteen traditions have cosmogonies that include void, oscillation, fourness, structured altered states, and relational binding — observations that comparative religion has been making for at least a century and that do not require a 12-dimensional ontology to explain. The cosmogony module's claim of cross-validation is, on the data the worldviews module itself contains, overstated. We recommend that the language of "independent cross-validation" be retracted in favour of the more accurate "informed by recurring patterns identified in comparative cosmological literature."

# 8. Future Work

Three lines of work follow naturally from this analysis. The first is *adversarial collaboration with tradition-keepers*. The encoding's most pressing deficiency is the absence of any community input. A near-term step would be to identify, for each of the sixteen named traditions, at least one community-authored or community-endorsed source that can be cited inline at every cell that draws on that tradition; to invite tradition-keepers to review, qualify, or refuse specific cells; and to publish a record of what was changed in response. This is slow, expensive work, and it cannot be automated. But it is the only path that makes the encoding's current claims defensible.

The second line is *subtractive encoding*. The current encoding represents each tradition only by what it includes; it has no mechanism for representing what it omits. A more honest representation would explicitly model what the encoder cannot or will not capture: which cells are restricted knowledge, which terms have been simplified to the point of distortion, which practices are embodied in ways that text cannot represent, and which aspects of the tradition the encoder lacks the standing to name at all. A subtractive encoding would be longer than the present additive encoding, and it would visibly mark the boundaries of the encoder's authority. It would also be more useful as a research artifact, because it would allow downstream consumers to know which cells are reliable enough to act on.

The third line is *metric for tradition preservation*. If the encoding is to be used as a substrate for an agentic system in any meaningful sense, the system's outputs should be evaluable against the source traditions for fidelity. This requires a metric that can compare an agent's behaviour to what a community-recognised practitioner would do, and it requires the metric to be defined with the community rather than imposed on it. This is, again, slow and expensive work; it is also the only kind of evaluation that would transform the substrate claim from a rhetorical assertion into a falsifiable proposition.

# 9. Conclusion

The cohezion `WorldviewExplorer` is a sincere, internally consistent, and ambitious encoding of sixteen indigenous cosmologies as a parameterised state machine over a ten-step cosmogonic scaffold. As a piece of engineering, it is competent; as a comparative document, it makes some defensible claims (the universality of the void, of cyclic time, and of relational binding), some claims that admit alternative explanations (fourness as cognitive prior rather than ontological structure), and some claims that strain against the data the encoding itself contains (the SPIN information unit, the imposition of "dynamic equilibrium" on heterogeneous practices). As a substrate for an autonomous-agent system, the encoding's promise is currently unrealised: no inference machinery in the cohezion code base presently consumes the encoded traditions in a way that demonstrably affects agent behaviour. As an ethical artifact, the encoding falls short of the standards now common in indigenous-data-sovereignty work: it secures no consent, cites no primary sources, and offers no reciprocity. We have argued throughout that the encoding is most usefully read as a research probe whose value lies in the questions it makes unavoidable rather than in the answers it asserts. The strongest single recommendation we can make to the cohezion project is that it retract the language of "independent cross-validation" in `cosmogony.py:55-65` and replace it with a more accurate description of the encoding's evidentiary status, and that any future development of the worldviews module begin from a relationship with the source communities rather than from a refinement of the schema.
