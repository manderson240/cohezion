## Hypothesis

The 5.5 million trajectories generated by the overnight [[universe-simulation|N-body simulation]] run would contain statistically meaningful structure -- not random noise -- and that characterizing this structure would reveal patterns applicable to [[agent-journey-tracking|agent trajectory analysis]]. Specifically, the hypothesis predicted that trajectory distributions would show clustering in position-velocity phase space, that convergence behaviors would vary by initial conditions, and that the data quality would be sufficient for downstream [[machine-learning|machine learning]] tasks (VAE training, predictive throttling) without additional synthetic augmentation.

## Method

1. **Data loading**: Ingested the 5.5M trajectory dataset from the overnight simulation output directory. Each trajectory consisted of position (3D), velocity (3D), and derived metrics (smoothness, convergence, energy) at multiple timesteps.
2. **Statistical profiling**: Computed distribution statistics for all trajectory dimensions: mean, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, and range. Checked for NaN/Inf values and data corruption.
3. **Clustering analysis**: Applied density-based clustering to identify natural trajectory families in phase space. Measured inter-cluster and intra-cluster distances.
4. **Temporal analysis**: Examined how trajectory properties evolved over simulation time: whether early-time trajectories differed systematically from late-time ones (indicating simulation drift or equilibration).
5. **Quality assessment**: Evaluated whether the data met the requirements for VAE training input (sufficient diversity, no degenerate modes, adequate coverage of the state space).
6. **Comparison with synthetic data**: Contrasted the real simulation data against the Gaussian noise synthetic data that had been used previously as a VAE training placeholder (see [[2026-02-23-never-train-vae-on-random-noise-as-synthetic-data]]).

## Results

- **Data integrity**: All 5.5M trajectories loaded successfully with zero NaN/Inf values and no corruption.
- **Distribution structure**: Trajectories were not uniformly distributed. Position values showed 3-4 distinct density peaks corresponding to gravitational wells (bound clusters). Velocity distributions showed a Maxwell-Boltzmann-like tail with excess kurtosis, consistent with gravitational scattering events.
- **Clustering**: Density-based clustering identified 8-12 trajectory families, with the largest family (gravitationally bound orbits) containing ~60% of trajectories and the smallest (ejected particles) containing ~5%.
- **Temporal evolution**: Early-time trajectories (first 10% of simulation) showed transient behavior from initial conditions. Post-equilibration trajectories were statistically stable, confirming the simulation reached a quasi-steady state.
- **VAE suitability**: The data exceeded minimum diversity requirements by a large margin. The 8-12 natural clusters provided structured variation that a VAE could learn to encode, unlike the previous synthetic Gaussian noise which had no learnable structure.
- **Real vs. synthetic comparison**: Real data showed 10x higher mutual information between dimensions compared to synthetic Gaussian data, confirming that real simulation data contains learnable correlations that synthetic data lacks.

## Analysis

The characterization confirmed that overnight simulation data is qualitatively different from synthetic placeholders. The [[morphospace-stability-wells|stability wells]] visible in the trajectory clustering correspond to physical attractors in the N-body system -- regions where gravitational binding creates persistent orbital families. These attractors have direct analogs in agent trajectory space: stable behavioral modes where agents reliably converge to consistent performance.

The temporal analysis was critical: the first ~10% of simulation time represents transient behavior that should be excluded from training data, as it reflects initial condition artifacts rather than system dynamics. This finding directly impacts how the [[2026-02-24-overnight-simulation-55m-12d-trajectories|12D trajectory embedding]] experiment should preprocess its input.

## Learnings

1. **Real simulation data has structure that synthetic data lacks**: The 10x mutual information difference between real and synthetic data means that models trained on synthetic data are learning to reconstruct noise, not patterns. The [[2026-02-23-never-train-vae-on-random-noise-as-synthetic-data]] decision was validated.
2. **Overnight runs produce equilibrated data**: After the initial transient, the simulation reaches a quasi-steady state where statistical properties are stable. This means overnight runs are long enough for production-quality training data.
3. **Natural clustering provides labels for free**: The 8-12 trajectory families identified by density clustering can serve as pseudo-labels for semi-supervised learning, eliminating the need for manual annotation.
4. **Transient exclusion is essential**: The first 10% of trajectories should be excluded from training pipelines to avoid contaminating learned representations with initial-condition artifacts.
5. **5.5M trajectories is more than sufficient**: Even after transient exclusion and quality filtering, the remaining ~4.5M trajectories far exceed the minimum for VAE training (typically ~100K for a 12D latent space).

## Relevance to Cohezion

This characterization experiment is the data quality gate for Cohezion's entire trajectory-based learning pipeline. The [[predictive-throttling-via-12d-trajectory-velocity|predictive throttling]] pattern, the [[latent-coherence-stability-predictor-lcsp|LCSP stability predictor]], and the VAE training pipeline all depend on the simulation producing structured, high-quality trajectory data. This experiment provided the evidence that the data meets those requirements, enabling the downstream experiments to proceed with confidence that their input data is sound.
