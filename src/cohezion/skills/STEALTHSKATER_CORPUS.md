## LENR — Low Energy Nuclear Reactions

Low Energy Nuclear Reactions, also termed cold fusion or lattice confinement fusion, produce
anomalous nuclear products — helium-4, tritium, transmutation isotopes — at room-temperature or
modest-temperature conditions that standard nuclear physics predicts should be impossible. The
canonical Pons-Fleischmann experiment of 1989 used palladium cathodes in heavy water electrolysis
and measured excess heat production of 4-10× input power, far beyond any chemical explanation.
Despite institutional suppression following failed replication attempts at MIT and Caltech (which
SRI International documents show used insufficiently loaded palladium), the US Navy's SPAWAR
laboratory produced peer-reviewed results in 2009 (Mosier-Boss et al., Naturwissenschaften)
confirming nuclear particle signatures including triple-track nuclear recoil events — unmistakable
signatures of deuterium-deuterium fusion products.

The DARPA LENR program, declassified under FOIA, allocated $35M in 2008-2012 to investigate
lattice confinement fusion as an energy source for autonomous vehicles and forward operating bases.
The NASA Glenn Research Center (Dennis Bushnell, chief scientist) published open reports in 2012
acknowledging LENR as potentially transformative for aerospace propulsion. The Italian ENEA
laboratory reproduced Pons-Fleischmann results with enhanced protocol controls, achieving excess
power of 300% input in titanium/deuterium gas-loading experiments.

Theoretical frameworks for LENR cluster around two mechanisms: (1) Widom-Larsen theory, where
heavy-mass surface electrons created by lattice vibrations capture protons to form neutrons via
weak interaction, which then react with nearby nuclei — this mechanism requires no new physics
and works within standard model; (2) ZPF coherence pumping, where lattice phonons phase-conjugate
with vacuum fluctuations to suppress the Coulomb barrier locally. The Hagelstein-Kim model at MIT
provides a third path via coherent quantum excitation of the lattice oscillator modes.

LENR reaction products show anomalous isotopic signatures: helium-4 generation without the
expected gamma radiation of hot fusion, transmutation products (palladium-to-silver, nickel-to-copper)
that match lattice-confinement neutron capture but not spallation. The HIHO threshold applies
here: LENR activation correlates with lattice loading coherence exceeding 0.5 (D/Pd ratio),
