### Adversarial Findings & Verdict
**1. FiLM Modulation:** 
The equation $h_{mod} = \gamma(x) \cdot h + \beta(x)$ lacks explicit bounds on $\gamma(x)$ and $\beta(x)$. If these parameters are generated by an unbounded network, the Jacobian $\partial h_{mod}/\partial h = \gamma(x)$ can cause gradient explosion during backpropagation. It requires bounded activations (e.g., sigmoid/tanh) or explicit weight normalization. *Status: ADVISORY.*

**2. Euclidean Distance Metric:** 
The metric $d = \|\Delta p \odot s\|$ defines a Riemannian metric with tensor $G = \text{diag}(dx^2, dy^2, dz^2)$. Under anisotropic voxel scaling ($dx \neq dy \neq dz$), this distance is *not* invariant under standard Euclidean rotations $SO(3)$. It is only invariant under the transformed rotation group $s^{-1}SO(3)s$. Thus, it fundamentally breaks Euclidean rotational invariance. *Status: FAIL.*

**3. ARC Integer Scale Test:** 
The strict membership test $h_{out}/h_{in} \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is highly vulnerable to floating-point representation errors (e.g., a true scale of $3.0$ evaluating to $2.9999999$). It requires an epsilon-tolerance check, such as $|h_{out}/h_{in} - \text{round}(h_{out}/h_{in})| < \epsilon$. *Status: ADVISORY.*

**Final Verdict: FAIL** 
The system contains a fundamental geometric violation (Q2) regarding rotational invariance under anisotropic scaling, alongside unaddressed numerical stability risks in Q1 and Q3.
