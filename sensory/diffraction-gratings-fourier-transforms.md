## Abstract

Diffraction gratings physically perform Fourier transforms on light, connecting optical physics directly to signal processing mathematics. The diffraction pattern represents a frequency decomposition of sample features through the fundamental relationship between Huygens' principle and Fourier analysis.

## Key Findings

- Fraunhofer diffraction patterns are mathematically equivalent to Fourier transforms of the aperture or obstacle function
- Diffraction represents a frequency decomposition where multiple light sources from a sample interfere according to superposition principles, creating Fourier-like transformations
- Lens-based optical systems enable signal processing applications including spatial filtering, optical correlation, and computer-generated holograms
- Fourier transform properties in optics enable diverse applications in imaging, information processing, beam shaping, and holography
