# Oman Signs the Artemis Accords — 61st Nation

## Summary

On January 26, 2026, the Sultanate of Oman signed the Artemis Accords during a ceremony in Muscat, becoming the 61st nation to commit to responsible space exploration for the benefit of all humanity. The signing deepens the U.S.-Oman partnership in space, science, and emerging technologies and reflects the growing international consensus around norms for lunar and deep space activities.

## Background: The Artemis Accords

The Artemis Accords were established in 2020 during the first Trump Administration, when the United States (led by NASA and the U.S. Department of State) joined with seven founding nations to create a practical framework for civil space exploration. The accords responded to growing interest in lunar activities by both governments and private companies, introducing the first set of principles aimed at enhancing safety, transparency, and coordination on the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

### Core Principles
Signatory nations commit to:
- **Peaceful exploration** — space activities conducted without conflict
- **Transparency** — open sharing of policies, plans, and scientific data
- **Interoperability** — compatible systems across international missions
- **Emergency assistance** — rendering aid to personnel in distress
- **Registration of space objects** — per existing international frameworks
- **Scientific data sharing** — making results available to the global community
- **Heritage preservation** — protecting historically significant sites and artifacts (e.g., Apollo landing sites)
- **Space resource utilization** — extraction and use of space resources in accordance with the Outer Space Treaty
- **Deconfliction** — preventing interference with other nations' activities
- **Orbital debris mitigation** — responsible management of space debris

## The Signing Ceremony

The ceremony was held in Muscat, attended by Oman's Minister of Transport, Communications, and Information Technology, Saeed Al-Maawali, and witnessed by U.S. Ambassador to Oman Ana Escrogima and NASA Deputy Associate Administrator Casey Swails. The signing coincided with the opening of the Middle East Space Conference and the U.S.-Oman Strategic Dialogue.

## Growing International Coalition

As of January 26, 2026, 61 countries have signed the Accords:

The rapid growth from 8 founding nations (2020) to 61 signatories (2026) indicates accelerating international consensus around the Accords framework. Notable holdouts include China and Russia, who have pursued their own International Lunar Research Station (ILRS) partnership.

## Significance

- **Middle East engagement**: Oman's signing represents continued expansion of the Accords into the Gulf region, alongside UAE and Saudi Arabia
- **Geopolitical dimension**: The Accords serve as a soft-power mechanism for U.S. space diplomacy, creating a coalition aligned with Western norms for space governance
- **Commercial space alignment**: The Accords' provisions on space resource utilization provide a legal framework that commercial lunar mining companies need for investment certainty
- **Artemis program momentum**: The diplomatic coalition supports NASA's operational Artemis program ([[artemis-ii-crewed-lunar-mission]]) by ensuring international cooperation on future lunar surface missions
