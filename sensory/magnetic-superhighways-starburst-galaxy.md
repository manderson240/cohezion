# Magnetic Superhighways in Starburst Galaxy Winds

## Summary

Published in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* on January 27, 2026, this paper by Enrique Lopez-Rodriguez (University of South Carolina) and collaborators presents the first detection of strongly magnetized molecular outflows in a starburst galaxy. Using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), the team mapped the magnetic field structure of Arp 220 -- an ultraluminous infrared galaxy (ULIRG) formed from two spiral galaxies in the final stages of merging, located approximately 250 million light-years away.

The observations revealed a "magnetic superhighway" in the western nucleus: a nearly vertical magnetic field running alongside a bipolar molecular outflow moving at approximately 500 km/s (1.1 million mph), driving metals, dust, and cosmic rays into the circumgalactic medium. In the eastern nucleus, ALMA found a spiral-like magnetic pattern threading a compact, dust-enshrouded disk and arm, demonstrating that ordered spiral magnetic fields can survive deep into the merger stage. A highly polarized dust bridge between the two nuclei (3-5% polarization fraction) traces a magnetized ridge potentially funneling material and magnetic flux between the merging cores.

Because Arp 220 is the nearest ULIRG, it serves as a local analog to the extreme, dusty star-forming galaxies that dominated the early universe more than 10 billion years ago. The findings suggest that strong, organized magnetic fields may be a universal feature of high-redshift starbursts, acting as a previously unrecognized driver of galactic wind feedback that regulates star formation across cosmic time.

## Key Findings

- **Magnetic superhighway**: A nearly vertical magnetic field in the west nucleus channels a bipolar molecular outflow at approximately 500 km/s, acting as invisible guardrails for material ejection
- **Extreme field strengths**: Estimated magnetic field strengths of 1-10 milligauss in the molecular outflows -- hundreds to thousands of times stronger than the Milky Way's average disk field -- implying compression and turbulence amplification
- **Surviving spiral fields**: The eastern nucleus retains an ordered spiral magnetic pattern within a compact disk, showing that organized fields persist even in violent merger environments
- **Magnetized dust bridge**: A 3-5% polarization fraction ridge between the two nuclei traces magnetized material transfer between merging cores
- **First CO(3-2) line polarization in an outflow**: The team reports the first detection of CO(3-2) emission line polarization via the Goldreich-Kylafis effect in a galactic outflow, a new diagnostic for magnetic field geometry

## Methodology

The team obtained full-polarization ALMA observations at 870 microns (Band 7), measuring both dust continuum polarization and CO(3-2) line polarization at a resolution of approximately 0.24 arcseconds (roughly 96 parsecs). This resolution was fine enough to spatially separate the two compact nuclei and their outflows. Dust continuum polarization traces the magnetic field orientation projected onto the plane of the sky (perpendicular to dust grain alignment), while the CO line polarization via the Goldreich-Kylafis effect provides an independent constraint on field geometry within the molecular gas. Magnetic field strengths were estimated from the degree of polarization and gas density models.

## Implications

Galaxy mergers and starbursts have long been known to launch powerful winds that regulate or quench star formation by removing gas. These results demonstrate that magnetic fields are a crucial, previously underappreciated driver in these winds. If magnetic superhighways are common in high-redshift starbursts (as Arp 220 suggests), they would significantly affect models of galactic chemical evolution, metal enrichment of the intergalactic medium, and cosmic ray propagation. The finding also provides new observational constraints for magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of galaxy mergers and feedback processes.
