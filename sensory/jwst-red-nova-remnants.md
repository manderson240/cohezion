# JWST Discovers Red Nova Merger Remnants Are Red Supergiants

## Summary

Using the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), astronomers have discovered for the first time what type of stellar body remains after two stars collide and merge in a luminous red nova. The result is a supermassive star similar to a red supergiant — hundreds of times the size of the Sun but much cooler (3,200-3,700 degrees C vs. the Sun's 5,700 degrees C). The team also found that these stellar mergers could have provided the raw materials needed for life. Published in January 2026.

## Key Findings

### The Nature of Merger Remnants
- When two stars merge in a luminous red nova, the resulting stellar body is a supermassive star resembling a red supergiant
- Surface temperatures range from 5,840 to 6,740 degrees F (3,200 to 3,700 degrees C) — significantly cooler than the Sun's ~10,300 degrees F (5,700 degrees C)
- If placed at the center of our solar system, these remnant stars would engulf the rocky inner planets and graze the orbit of Jupiter
- As the researchers noted: "Until now, it was unknown what type of star would remain after the merger"

### Observational Strategy
- Combined JWST infrared data gathered in 2023-2024 with Hubble visible light images and Spitzer Space Telescope data
- Observed two red nova remnants at different evolutionary stages:
  - **AT 2011kp**: 12 years after the stellar merger event
  - **AT 1997bs**: 27 years after the merger event
- Required waiting years after the initial merger for the ejected material shell to thin enough for infrared observation

### Why Prior Observations Were Impossible
When stars merge to create a luminous red nova, they eject a vast amount of stellar material — the bright, dense shell of ejected matter produces the brightest phase of the transient but simultaneously obscures the view of the created stellar body. Every luminous red nova can eject dust equivalent to 300 times the mass of Earth. Only as this material expands and thins over years to decades does the underlying merger remnant become observable, particularly in JWST's infrared wavelengths that can penetrate dust.

### Implications for Life's Building Blocks
The stellar material ejected during red nova events enriches the surrounding interstellar medium with heavy elements. The team found evidence that these mergers could have contributed the raw materials (carbon, oxygen, and heavier elements) needed for the formation of life-bearing planetary systems.

## Methodology

The study leveraged JWST's MIRI (Mid-Infrared Instrument) and NIRCam capabilities to penetrate the dust shells surrounding merger remnants. By observing at infrared wavelengths where dust is transparent, the team was able to:

1. Identify the spectral characteristics of the underlying stellar body
2. Measure surface temperature through spectral energy distribution fitting
3. Estimate the physical size of the merger remnant
4. Characterize the composition of the ejected material shell

The multi-epoch approach (observing remnants at 12 and 27 years post-merger) provided evolutionary context, showing how merger remnants evolve over decades.

## Context: Red Novae and Stellar Mergers

Red novae (also called luminous red novae) are a class of stellar transient events caused by the merger of two stars, typically in a binary system where orbital decay eventually brings the stars into contact. They are distinct from classical novae (thermonuclear explosions on white dwarfs) and supernovae (stellar core collapse). Six Galactic and over a dozen extragalactic red novae are known. JWST Cycle 4 proposals include continued study of red novae as probes of binary stellar evolution and merger physics.
