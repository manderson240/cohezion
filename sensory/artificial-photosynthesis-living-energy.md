## Abstract

Artificial photosynthesis systems convert sunlight into chemical fuels by mimicking natural photosynthesis using photoelectrochemical cells or living organisms like cyanobacteria. This technology offers sustainable energy storage without bulky batteries, converting water and CO2 into usable fuels.

## Key Findings

- Photoelectrochemical cells absorb light and split water into hydrogen and oxygen or reduce CO2 into carbon-based fuels using solar energy
- Living systems like green microalgae and cyanobacteria can be engineered as photosynthetic biohybrid systems combining semiconductors and bacteria
- These systems absorb solar energy and create liquid fuels from water and CO2 while releasing oxygen, matching natural photosynthesis outputs
- Commercial artificial photosynthesis systems may become viable within 10-15 years, providing dramatic improvements in clean energy deployment
