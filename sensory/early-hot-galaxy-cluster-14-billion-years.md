## Summary
Astronomers discovered the earliest known galaxy cluster with blazing hot intra-cluster gas, existing just 1.4 billion years after the Big Bang — far earlier and hotter than current theoretical models predict possible.

## Key Abstractions
Published in Nature, this UBC-led discovery challenges standard models of galaxy cluster formation which predict such extreme temperatures only occur in mature, stable clusters billions of years later. The anomalous early heating mechanism remains unexplained, potentially requiring revisions to cosmological structure formation theory including AGN feedback models and early dark energy scenarios.
