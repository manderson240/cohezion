# Mars Confirms Einstein's Time Dilation

## Summary

NIST physicists Neil Ashby and Bijunath R. Patla published the first precise calculation of time dilation between Earth and Mars in *The Astronomical Journal* (December 1, 2025). Their analysis confirms Einstein's general relativity prediction: clocks on Mars run faster than on Earth because Mars sits in a weaker gravitational field, with surface gravity approximately one-fifth of Earth's.

The headline result is that a clock on Mars ticks 477 microseconds (millionths of a second) faster than an Earth clock per day, on average. However, Mars's eccentric orbit and gravitational influences from the Sun, Earth, and Moon cause this figure to vary by as much as 226 microseconds over the course of a Martian year. The calculation required solving a four-body problem (Sun, Earth, Moon, Mars) -- significantly more complex than the three-body calculations previously done for lunar timekeeping.

These findings have immediate practical implications for future Mars missions. Current 5G networks rely on microsecond-level synchronization, and GPS satellites are synchronized to a few nanoseconds. For Mars, the 477-microsecond daily offset would accumulate to navigation errors of approximately 89 miles (143 km) per day if uncorrected, making dynamic, continuously adjusted timing corrections essential for any Mars satellite constellation or communication relay system.

## Key Findings

- **477 microseconds per day**: On average, a Mars clock runs ahead of an Earth clock by this amount due to weaker gravitational field
- **226-microsecond variability**: Mars's orbital eccentricity and gravitational perturbations from the Sun, Earth, and Moon cause daily variation of up to 226 microseconds over a Martian year
- **Four-body problem**: The calculation required accounting for gravitational effects from the Sun, Earth, Moon, and Mars simultaneously -- "the heavy lifting was more challenging than I initially thought," noted Patla
- **9 seconds over 50 years**: A human living on Mars for 50 Earth years would age approximately 9 seconds more than a counterpart on Earth
- **Comparison**: Moon clocks run 56 microseconds faster per day; GPS satellites run 38 microseconds faster -- Mars dilation is an order of magnitude larger than GPS corrections

## Methodology

The researchers established a reference point on the Martian surface analogous to sea level at the equator on Earth. Using decades of gravity data collected from Mars missions (including landers and orbiters), they estimated surface gravity precisely. The time dilation calculation factored in gravitational potential differences (general relativistic gravitational time dilation) and velocity effects (special relativistic time dilation from orbital motion). The four-body gravitational interaction between the Sun, Earth, Moon, and Mars required numerical integration techniques beyond the analytical approaches sufficient for two- or three-body problems. This work builds on their 2024 paper developing precise timekeeping plans for the Moon.

## Implications

As humanity prepares for sustained Mars operations -- including NASA's Artemis program and future crewed missions -- relativistic time corrections become engineering requirements rather than theoretical curiosities. Any Mars satellite constellation will need continuously updated timing corrections that account for orbital position, unlike GPS which uses a simpler static clock adjustment. The work establishes the foundational metrology for "Mars time" that will be needed for navigation, communication, and eventually for any Mars-based positioning system analogous to GPS.
