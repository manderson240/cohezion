# Biefeld-Brown Effect: Electrogravitics and Jefimenko's Causal Fields

## Summary

Tom Valone reframes the Biefeld-Brown electrogravitic effect through Jefimenko's source-based ("causal") electromagnetic formulation. The key insight: the effect lives in the **transient** (the edge, the rate of current change), not the steady-state voltage. This shifts the engineering problem from "higher voltage" to "sharper edges" and "creative waveshaping."

## The Causal EM Framework

Standard EM teaching says "changing magnetic field causes electric field" and vice versa. Jefimenko insists this is an incomplete causal picture:

> *"This is the essence of electromagnetic induction, as Maxwell intended, which is measured by, not caused by, a changing magnetic field."* — Oleg Jefimenko

The correct picture: charges and currents (sources) generate fields that propagate outward. Induction is about what the sources are doing, where, and **how quickly**.

## The Third Term: Electrokinetic Force

Jefimenko's causal formulation of the electric field has three terms. The third — the **electrokinetic contribution** — is Valone's pivot:

1. It is **directed along the direction of current**
2. It **exists only while current is changing in time**

This temporal gate transforms electrogravitic thrust from a steady push into a **switching phenomenon** — appearing at turn-on, turn-off, or rapid transitions.

## The Engineering Lever: Rise Time

> *"An important consequence is that the faster the rates of change of current, the larger will be the electrokinetic force. Therefore, high voltage pulsed inputs are favored."* — Tom Valone

**Rise time becomes the amplifier.** The design lever is dI/dt (rate of current change), not steady voltage. This explains why:
- Flyback transformers (asymmetric sawtooth waveforms with sharp transitions) produce "better results" than smooth sine waves
- Television flyback folklore is narratively meaningful in this framework
- High voltage helps only because it buys fast current transients

## The Cancellation Problem

The mechanism has a built-in trap: the declining portion of a surge creates an opposing force. Naive pulses **self-cancel**.

**Solution**: Creative waveshaping (Schlicher waveform):
- Rectified current surge with very steep leading edge
- Slowly declining trailing edge
- Extended negative-current phase that reinforces the electrokinetic contribution
- Net unidirectional contribution for much of the cycle

> *"Very fast changes in current has to produce the most dynamic electrokinetic force. Creative waveshaping seems to be the answer."* — Tom Valone

## Falsifiable Predictions

The framework generates testable scaling expectations:
- **Force should track edge speed** more reliably than steady voltage
- **Symmetric waveforms** (maximizing cancellation) should collapse any net impulse
- **Asymmetric waveforms** should produce stronger directional signatures
- **Proportional voltage/current rise** required — inflating peak voltage while flattening current profile reduces performance

## Replication Requirements

A credible test needs:
- Full waveform capture at device terminals
- Impedance characterization
- Geometry disclosure and material properties
- Separation of electrical transients from mechanical coupling
- Voltage slew and current slew must remain proportional
