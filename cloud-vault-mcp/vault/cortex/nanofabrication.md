## Definition

Construction of functional structures at nanometer scales (1-100 nm) using bottom-up approaches. Pioneered by Nadrian Seeman's invention of DNA nanotechnology (1982) and Paul Rothemund's DNA origami method (Nature 2006) for folding single-stranded DNA into arbitrary 2D shapes with 6 nm resolution using 200+ oligonucleotide staple strands.

## Key Properties

- DNA origami folds 7+ kilobase scaffolds using 200+ staple strands into arbitrary shapes at 6 nm resolution
- Bottom-up assembly allows atomic-level precision for metal nanoparticles, photonic devices, and biological components
- 2025 breakthrough: 2D DNA origami lattices on silicon reach 5.6 um^2 single-crystal formations
- Applications span targeted drug delivery, biosensing, nanophotonics, and synthetic cell engineering
- Multiplexed detection under complex biological conditions through spatial organization

## Examples

- Rothemund 2006: DNA folded into smiley faces, snowflakes, and Americas map at 100 nm scale
- 2025: Smart DNA origami nanofluidic systems for targeted cancer therapy delivering compounds to tumor cells
