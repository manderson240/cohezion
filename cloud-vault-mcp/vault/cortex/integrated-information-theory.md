# Integrated Information Theory (IIT)

## Definition

Integrated Information Theory (IIT), developed by Giulio Tononi (2004, 2008, 2014), is a mathematical theory of consciousness that identifies consciousness with **integrated information** — the amount of information generated by a system above and beyond the information generated by its parts independently. The theory's central quantity, Φ (phi), measures how much a system is "more than the sum of its parts" informationally.

IIT is the only current theory of consciousness that is:
1. **Mathematical** — Φ is a precisely defined quantity computable (in principle) for any system
2. **Falsifiable** — it makes specific predictions about which physical systems are conscious and which are not
3. **Panpsychist (in structure)** — any system with Φ > 0 has some degree of experience, from electrons to ecosystems

This makes IIT the natural bridge between physics and consciousness studies, and a key component of the TOE framework: if AUM = consciousness = the ground state, then Φ measures how much of AUM's intrinsic nature is realized in a given physical system.

## Key Properties

### The Five Axioms (Phenomenological)

IIT begins with axioms about experience (not physics):

1. **Intrinsic existence** — experience exists from the intrinsic perspective of the system
2. **Composition** — experience is structured (has many distinguishable components)
3. **Information** — experience is specific (this experience, not that one)
4. **Integration** — experience is unified (not decomposable into independent parts)
5. **Exclusion** — experience is definite (has specific borders and grain)

### The Postulates (Physical)

Each axiom maps to a physical postulate:

| Axiom | Postulate | Physical Requirement |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| Intrinsic existence | Cause-effect power | System must have causal power over itself |
| Composition | All subsets | All subsets of elements must be considered |
| Information | Selectivity | System must be in one specific state among alternatives |
| Integration | Irreducibility | System's cause-effect structure must be irreducible (Φ > 0) |
| Exclusion | Maximality | Only the maximum of Φ over all possible partitions and spatial/temporal grains counts |

### Φ — Integrated Information

For a system S with elements {e₁, e₂, ..., eₙ} in state s:

1. Compute the **cause-effect structure** — the full set of cause-effect repertoires for all subsets of elements
2. For each partition of S into parts, compute the **earth mover's distance** (EMD) between the whole system's cause-effect structure and the partitioned system's
3. **Φ** = the minimum EMD over all possible partitions (the **minimum information partition**, MIP)

> Φ = min_{partition P} EMD(CE(S), CE(S|P))

If Φ = 0, the system can be fully explained by its parts → not conscious (as a whole).
If Φ > 0, the system generates information above and beyond its parts → conscious (to degree Φ).

### Predictions

IIT makes counterintuitive predictions that distinguish it from other theories:

| System | IIT Prediction | Functionalist Prediction |
|--------|---------------|------------------------|
| Cerebellum (80B neurons, feedforward) | Low Φ (not conscious) | High (lots of computation) |
| Cerebral cortex (20B neurons, recurrent) | High Φ (conscious) | High (lots of computation) |
| Digital computer (serial, feedforward) | Very low Φ (not conscious) | Could be conscious if right program |
| Photodiode | Φ > 0 (minimally conscious) | Not conscious (too simple) |
| Split-brain patient | Two separate Φ maxima | One consciousness with access issues |

The cerebellum prediction has empirical support: cerebellar lesions don't abolish consciousness, while cortical lesions do — despite the cerebellum having 4× more neurons.

## Mathematical Framework

### Cause-Effect Repertoire

For a mechanism m (subset of elements) in state s, the cause repertoire is the probability distribution over past states that could have caused s:

> p(past | m = s) via Bayes' theorem with maximum entropy priors

The effect repertoire is the probability distribution over future states that m in state s will produce:

> p(future | m = s) via the system's transition probability matrix (TPM)

The cause-effect information of mechanism m:

> ce(m) = min(ci(m), ei(m))

where ci and ei are the irreducible cause and effect information (EMD from the unconstrained distribution after the MIP).

### Computational Complexity

Computing Φ exactly is computationally intractable: it requires evaluating all 2ⁿ subsets of elements and all possible bipartitions. The complexity is super-exponential:

> Time complexity: O(2^(2^n))

For n > ~15 elements, exact computation is infeasible. This has motivated approximations (Φ*, Φ_AR, geometric integrated information) and sparked debate about whether computational intractability undermines the theory's empirical testability.
