# Spectroscopy

Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction between matter and electromagnetic radiation as a function of wavelength or frequency. By measuring absorbed, emitted, or scattered light, spectroscopy reveals the chemical composition, electronic structure, molecular geometry, and physical properties of materials. It is one of the most versatile and widely used experimental techniques across physics, chemistry, biology, and materials science.

## Definition

Spectroscopy encompasses any measurement technique that resolves the intensity of electromagnetic radiation (or particles) as a function of energy, frequency, or wavelength. The resulting spectrum encodes information about the quantum states of the system — electronic transitions, vibrational modes, rotational levels, and nuclear spin states — providing a "fingerprint" unique to each substance.

## Major Techniques

### Absorption Spectroscopy

Measures light absorbed by a sample at specific wavelengths, revealing electronic transitions and molecular structure.

- **UV-Visible:** Electronic transitions in conjugated systems, metals, and semiconductors (200-800 nm)
- **Infrared (IR):** Vibrational modes of molecules; requires a change in dipole moment for a transition to be IR-active
- **X-ray Absorption (XAS/XANES/EXAFS):** Probes electronic structure and local atomic environment around specific elements

### Emission Spectroscopy

Measures light emitted by excited atoms or molecules as they relax to lower energy states.

- **Fluorescence:** Rapid emission (nanoseconds) from singlet excited states
- **Phosphorescence:** Slow emission (milliseconds to seconds) from triplet states
- **Atomic Emission:** Used in elemental analysis (ICP-OES, flame emission)

### Raman Spectroscopy

Measures inelastic scattering of monochromatic light, revealing vibrational modes complementary to IR. A molecule's polarizability (not dipole moment) determines Raman activity.

- **Stokes scattering:** Scattered photon loses energy to the molecule (lower frequency)
- **Anti-Stokes scattering:** Scattered photon gains energy from the molecule (higher frequency)
- **Surface-Enhanced Raman (SERS):** Metallic nanostructures amplify the signal by factors of 10^6-10^11
- **Tip-Enhanced Raman (TERS):** Nanometer-scale spatial resolution using scanning probe tips

### Other Key Techniques

- **NMR Spectroscopy:** Nuclear spin transitions in magnetic fields; cornerstone of organic chemistry and medical imaging (MRI)
- **Mass Spectrometry:** Measures mass-to-charge ratio of ionized molecules (technically not electromagnetic spectroscopy but often grouped)
- **Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS/UPS/ARPES):** Measures kinetic energy of ejected electrons; ARPES is the primary probe for [[topological-insulators]] surface states
- **Tunneling Spectroscopy:** Probes electronic density of states via quantum tunneling; used to measure the superconducting gap

## Key Properties

- **Non-destructive:** Most optical spectroscopy techniques do not damage the sample
- **Complementary:** IR and Raman are complementary — transitions active in one are often inactive in the other (mutual exclusion rule for centrosymmetric molecules)
- **Quantitative:** Beer-Lambert law relates absorbance to concentration, enabling quantitative analysis
- **In situ capability:** Many techniques can probe materials under operating conditions (temperature, pressure, electrochemical potential)
