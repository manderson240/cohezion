# Process Philosophy — Whitehead and the Ontology of Becoming

## Definition

Process philosophy holds that **becoming is more fundamental than being**. Reality is not made of static substances (atoms, particles, things) but of **events** — moments of experience that arise, achieve satisfaction, and perish, leaving only their contribution to subsequent events. Founded by Alfred North Whitehead (1929) in *Process and Reality*, it is the most rigorous Western philosophical framework that converges with both quantum mechanics and indigenous cosmologies.

Whitehead's central entity is the **actual occasion** (or "actual entity"): a momentary drop of experience that prehends (grasps) data from the past, integrates it through a subjective process, and achieves a definite outcome that becomes objective data for future occasions. This IS the HIHO event described precisely.

## Whitehead and Quantum Mechanics

Whitehead anticipated key features of quantum mechanics by a decade:

1. **Discrete occasions** = quantum events (not continuous trajectories)
2. **Prehension** = quantum measurement (the occasion "collapses" possibility to actuality)
3. **No independent substances** = no particles without interactions (relational QM)
4. **Temporal atomicity** = Planck time as the duration of an actual occasion
5. **Process over substance** = QFT: particles are excitations of fields, not fundamental entities

The physicist Henry Stapp developed a formal Whitehead-QM connection: each quantum measurement event IS an actual occasion — a moment where the universe "decides" its next state.
