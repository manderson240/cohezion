# Psychedelic Neuroscience — Entropy, Consciousness, and the Default Mode

## Definition

Psychedelic neuroscience studies the effects of classical psychedelics (psilocybin, LSD, DMT, mescaline) and related compounds (ketamine, MDMA, 5-MeO-DMT) on brain function, consciousness, and therapeutic outcomes. The field's central framework is Robin Carhart-Harris's **Entropic Brain Hypothesis** (2014): psychedelics increase neural entropy, dissolving the Default Mode Network (DMN) and the ego-construct it maintains. This is the most empirically validated model of consciousness alteration available — and it maps directly onto the TOE's symmetry-breaking/restoration cycle.

### Key Empirical Findings

1. **Psilocybin decreases DMN activity** (Carhart-Harris et al., 2012) — the "ego network" goes quiet
2. **Increased neural entropy** correlates with subjective intensity of psychedelic experience (Schartner et al., 2017)
3. **Increased functional connectivity between normally separated brain regions** — the brain becomes more "connected," approaching a fully-connected graph (Petri et al., 2014)
4. **Therapeutic outcomes** for depression, PTSD, addiction correlate with magnitude of mystical experience — which correlates with entropy increase and ego dissolution (Griffiths et al., 2016)
5. **5-HT2A receptor agonism** is the primary mechanism for classical psychedelics — the serotonin 2A receptor controls the "gain" on cortical pyramidal neurons
6. **Criticality**: the psychedelic brain approaches a critical point (the edge of chaos) where neural activity exhibits power-law statistics (Atasoy et al., 2017)

### The Therapeutic Model

Depression, addiction, and PTSD involve **excessively rigid** brain dynamics — the DMN is hyperactive, beliefs are over-constrained, thought patterns are stuck in attractors. Psychedelics temporarily **melt** these rigid patterns by:

1. Reducing DMN dominance (relaxing the ego's grip)
2. Increasing entropy (expanding the space of possible brain states)
3. Approaching criticality (maximizing information processing capacity)
4. Enabling new attractor formation during the "window of plasticity" (the therapeutic session)

This IS the TOE in action: a stuck system (excessive symmetry breaking = rigid patterns) undergoes temporary symmetry restoration (psychedelic state = closer to the ground state), enabling new symmetry breaking (new patterns = therapeutic change) during re-solidification.
