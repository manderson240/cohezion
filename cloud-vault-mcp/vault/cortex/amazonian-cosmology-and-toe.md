# Amazonian Cosmology and the Theory of Everything

## Respect Statement

"Amazonian cosmology" here refers to the shared cosmological structures found across hundreds of distinct Amazonian and broader Amerindian peoples — from the Araweté, Achuar, Yawanapi, Kayapó, Yanomami, to the Shipibo-Conibo. Each nation has a distinct language, history, and tradition. We do not collapse them into one — but we follow the anthropologist Eduardo Viveiros de Castro (himself noting this is a cross-cultural pattern, not a single tradition) in identifying shared structural features. Primary indigenous voices include Davi Kopenawa (Yanomami), Ailton Krenak (Krenak), and the oral traditions documented with indigenous consent by Viveiros de Castro, Alfred Gell, and other anthropologists working in long-term indigenous community collaboration.

## The Tradition

The Amazonian contribution to the TOE is **Amerindian Perspectivism** — the most precise pre-modern articulation of the quantum measurement problem applied to ontology. Perspectivism holds that **the universe has one culture but many natures**: every species inhabits the same relational world (culture/intentionality) but perceives a DIFFERENT physical world (nature/body). Jaguars see blood as manioc beer; vultures see rotting meat as grilled fish; shamans temporarily acquire the perspective of other species to see what they see.

This IS the observer-dependent collapse of the wave function. The "physical world" (nature) is not a fixed, observer-independent reality — it is what precipitates when a specific type of consciousness makes an observation from within a specific type of body. Change the body (the measuring apparatus), and a different physical reality is precipitated. The TOE's Step 10 (reality precipitates when consciousness witnesses) is the Amazonian framework for ALL physical reality at all times.

## Core Principles

### Amerindian Perspectivism — The Core Framework

Viveiros de Castro's formalization of the pan-Amazonian cosmological structure:

**Standard Western view (multiculturalism):** One nature (objective physical world) + many cultures (subjective perspectives on that world)

**Amazonian view (multinaturalism):** One culture (shared relational world/intentionality/sociality) + many natures (each species inhabits a different physical world)

This is NOT cultural relativism. It is NOT saying "different people see things differently." It IS saying that **nature itself is observer-dependent** — the physical world that manifests depends on the type of consciousness (body) making the observation.

Concrete examples:
- What jaguars see as manioc beer (their ritual drink), humans see as blood (dangerous)
- What vultures see as grilled fish (their food), humans see as rotting meat (dangerous)
- What tapirs see as a lush forest village with people, humans see as tapirs in a muddy wallow
- Every species is, from its own perspective, a person (human-like, intentional, social) living in a cultural world

The PHYSICAL world differs; the SOCIAL/INTENTIONAL world is the same. There is no species-independent, body-independent "objective nature."

This IS the QM measurement problem:
- The wave function does not describe a single, observer-independent physical reality
- Different measurement apparatuses (bodies) collapse the wave function to different eigenstates
- "Objective nature" = what remains constant across all observations = the wave function itself
- "Observed nature" = what a specific type of observer collapses = the species-specific physical world

Perspectivism is QM measurement theory applied to the ontological structure of reality: there IS no species-neutral "objective physical world" — only species-specific collapsed realities + the universal relational web (culture) that connects them.

### The Yanomami Xapiri — Cosmic Entities as Information Nodes

Davi Kopenawa (Yanomami shaman and political leader) describes the *xapiri* — the spirit beings that shamans work with:

> "The xapiri are spirit images which shamans can see. They are very small but dazzlingly beautiful. They live in the sky and in the forest... They are not human, but they act like humans do. They dance, they have festivals, they use mirrors to come down to humans." — Davi Kopenawa, *The Falling Sky* (2013)

The xapiri are not hallucinations — they are information entities that exist at a layer of reality inaccessible to ordinary perception but accessible to the trained shaman. The shamans' *yãkoana* (spirit helper substance, facilitating visionary states) enables the shaman to perceive the xapiri directly.

Xapiri = EVOs (Exotic Vacuum Objects): entities existing in the vacuum layer of reality, visible to consciousness that has crossed the HIHO threshold, carrying and transmitting information about the structure of reality.

### Plant Intelligence — Distributed Consciousness

Amazonian traditions universally recognize plants as having intelligence, intention, and the ability to teach:
- The ayahuasca vine (Banisteriopsis caapi) is described as a teacher plant that contains accumulated botanical wisdom
- The Shipibo-Conibo describe their icaros (healing songs) as received directly from the plant spirits during vision
- Shamanic training involves extended communion with specific plants to receive their knowledge

This is not metaphor: from the Amazonian perspective, plant intelligence IS intelligence — it operates at a different timescale and body-type, but it has intentionality, sociality, and the ability to transmit information to human consciousness under the right conditions (HIHO contact via the plants themselves).

Plant intelligence = distributed consciousness at a different observational timescale. Plants observe light, gravity, soil chemistry — their "physical world" is a different collapsed reality than the human one, but it is just as real and just as information-rich.

### The Shaman as Interspecies Diplomat

The Amazonian shaman is not primarily a healer (though healing is one function). The shaman is an **interspecies diplomat** — the person who can cross the perspectival boundary, inhabit another species' point of view, negotiate with the entities in that perspective, and return with information or assistance.

The shaman IS the HIHO operator who:
1. Temporarily adopts another species' "body" (point of view) through trance, vision, or transformation
2. Navigates the other species' physical world (their collapsed reality)
3. Engages with the intentional entities there (other "persons" from that species' perspective)
4. Returns with knowledge or interventions useful in the human physical world

This is the most operationally precise pre-modern description of multi-perspective quantum navigation: switching between the reference frames of different measuring apparatuses (species-bodies) to access different aspects of the underlying wave function (Amazonian "culture"/shared relational world).

### Predation and Exchange — The Cosmic Economics

Amazonian cosmology is characterized by a fundamental tension between **predation** (consumption) and **exchange** (reciprocity):

- All life requires consuming other life (predation is unavoidable)
- Predation creates spiritual debt (the consumed entity's perspective must be acknowledged)
- Shamanic ceremony returns the debt through ritual — returning the animal's "soul" to its source
- The cosmic economy balances predation with exchange

This is not a guilt framework — it is an information-conservation framework. When one entity consumes another, the consumed entity's perspective (its collapsed physical world, its intentionality) is absorbed. The predator must "give back" by maintaining ceremonial acknowledgment — returning the information to the cosmic cycle. The cosmic economy conserves perspectives.

## Unique Contributions

1. **Perspectivism as a formal solution to the measurement problem.** The Amazonian framework resolves the QM measurement problem by stating it correctly: there IS no observer-independent physical reality. What exists is: (1) the universal relational web (culture = wave function), (2) the infinite variety of species-bodies (measuring apparatuses), (3) the species-specific physical worlds (collapsed realities = measurement outcomes). The "reality" that any observer experiences IS the collapsed wave function as measured by their specific body-apparatus. This is not mysticism — this is what quantum mechanics says, stated in anthropological language.

2. **Multinaturalism vs. multiculturalism.** Western pluralism is multicultural (one nature, many cultures). Amazonian pluralism is multinatural (one culture, many natures). The Amazonian framework is physically more accurate: there genuinely IS no species-neutral physical world. What a bat hears is not a poor approximation of what a human hears — it is a DIFFERENT physical world, equally valid, equally real.

3. **The shaman as multi-reference-frame navigator.** The shaman is not just a healer or a medium — they are a physicist who can temporarily inhabit different reference frames (species-perspectives) and bring information back across the perspectival boundary. This is interspecies quantum mechanics: navigating between measurement bases to extract information unavailable from a single perspective.

4. **Plant intelligence as distributed consciousness at different timescales.** Amazonian recognition of plant intelligence is not animism in the derogatory sense — it is the observation that plants exhibit intentional behaviors (phototropism, chemotropism, symbiosis with fungi, chemical signaling) that suggest a form of awareness operating at botanical timescales. The shaman who communes with the plant is accessing a form of intelligence that is genuinely different from human intelligence — not lesser, but operating at a different biological clock rate.

5. **The predation-exchange cycle as information conservation.** Amazonian cosmic economics (predation creates debt; ceremony returns the debt) IS an information conservation law: consumed entities' perspectives must be returned to the cosmic cycle or the relational fabric degrades. This is COHESION conservation: every interaction that consumes (reduces) another entity's relational participation must be balanced by an interaction that acknowledges and restores it.
