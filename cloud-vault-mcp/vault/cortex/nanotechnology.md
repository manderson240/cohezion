# Nanotechnology

## Definition

Nanotechnology is the manipulation, characterization, and application of materials and devices at the nanoscale -- dimensions ranging from approximately 1 to 100 nanometers. At this scale, materials exhibit unique physical, chemical, and optical properties that differ fundamentally from their bulk counterparts due to quantum mechanical effects and dramatically increased surface-to-volume ratios. Nanotechnology bridges physics, chemistry, biology, and engineering to create structures and devices with precisely controlled nanoscale features for applications ranging from medicine to electronics to energy.

## Key Properties

- **Size-dependent properties:** Nanomaterials display quantum confinement effects (semiconductor quantum dots emit size-tunable colors), enhanced catalytic activity (nanoparticles expose more surface atoms for reactions), and altered mechanical strength (carbon nanotubes have tensile strength ~100x that of steel at 1/6 the weight).
- **Nanomaterial classifications:** Materials are categorized by dimensionality: 0D (quantum dots, fullerenes), 1D (nanowires, carbon nanotubes), 2D (graphene, MoS2 monolayers), and 3D (nanocomposites, metal-organic frameworks). Lower dimensionality yields higher surface-to-volume ratios and more pronounced quantum effects.
- **Fabrication approaches:** Top-down methods (lithography, etching, milling) carve bulk materials to nanoscale features. Bottom-up methods (self-assembly, chemical vapor deposition, molecular beam epitaxy) build structures atom by atom. Green synthesis using biological agents is an emerging sustainable approach.
- **Characterization tools:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), and X-ray diffraction (XRD) enable imaging and measurement at the nanoscale. Cryo-electron microscopy has extended structural characterization to biological nanomaterials.
- **Safety and regulation:** Nanomaterials may exhibit different toxicological profiles than bulk materials. Particle size, shape, surface chemistry, and aggregation behavior all affect biological interactions, driving ongoing research into nanotoxicology and regulatory frameworks.

## Examples

- Graphene, a single-atom-thick carbon sheet, has the highest known electrical conductivity and mechanical strength among 2D materials, with applications in flexible electronics, energy storage, and composite materials.
- Lipid nanoparticles delivered the mRNA payload in COVID-19 vaccines (Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna), demonstrating nanotechnology's critical role in drug delivery and immunology.
- DNA origami enables precise nanoscale patterning of semiconductor and metallic structures by folding DNA strands into programmable 2D and 3D templates.
