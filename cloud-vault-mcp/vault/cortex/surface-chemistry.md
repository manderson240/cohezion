## Definition

Surface chemistry is the study of chemical reactions and physical phenomena occurring at the interface between two phases — solid/gas, solid/liquid, liquid/gas, or solid/solid. It encompasses adsorption, heterogeneous catalysis, wetting, friction, corrosion, and surface reconstruction. Because atoms at a surface have fewer bonding neighbours than those in the bulk, they exhibit distinct electronic structure and reactivity that govern how materials interact with their environment.

The field is central to technologies ranging from semiconductor fabrication and fuel cells to drug delivery and environmental remediation. Modern surface science combines ultra-high-vacuum spectroscopy (XPS, LEED, STM) with computational density-functional theory to map reaction pathways at atomic resolution, while emerging 2D materials and quantum-confined nanoclusters are opening new frontiers in catalytic interface design.

Surface chemistry also underpins the rapidly growing field of electrocatalysis for sustainable energy, where electrode surface composition and morphology determine selectivity in reactions such as CO2 reduction, water splitting, and nitrogen fixation.

## Key Properties

- **Adsorption energetics** — Physisorption (van der Waals, reversible) and chemisorption (covalent/ionic, often irreversible) determine catalytic selectivity, with binding strength following volcano-plot scaling relations (Sabatier principle)
- **Heterogeneous catalysis** — Reactants adsorb on the solid catalyst surface, undergo bond breaking and rearrangement, then desorb; four principal mechanisms operate (Langmuir-Hinshelwood, Mars-van-Krevelen, Langmuir-Rideal, and Eley-Rideal)
- **Superlubricity** — Emerges at incommensurate interfaces where surface lattices are out of registry, reducing friction to near zero (graphene-on-graphite is the canonical example)
- **Surface reconstruction and passivation** — Surface atoms rearrange to minimise free energy; engineered passivation layers (oxides, thiols, ligands) control stability under harsh conditions such as acid, heat, or oxidising environments
- **Quantum confinement at surfaces** — When surface-to-volume ratios become large (nanoclusters, quantum dots, 2D materials), electronic band structure and catalytic behaviour shift dramatically from bulk properties

## Examples

- **Superatom catalysis** — Cu45 nanoclusters display closed-shell superatomic electronic structure that enables highly selective CO2-to-ethylene electroreduction while surviving boiling water and concentrated acid
- **Photoelectrochemical water splitting** — Semiconductor surface band-edge engineering controls hole-transfer kinetics at the solid/liquid interface in artificial photosynthesis cells
- **Graphene superlubricity** — Incommensurate graphite polytype interfaces demonstrate structural superlubricity with switching controlled by nanocavity geometry
- **Single-atom catalysts** — Isolated metal atoms anchored on oxide supports maximise catalytic surface area and achieve near-100 % atom efficiency
