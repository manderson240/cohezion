## Definition

Optical properties describe how materials interact with electromagnetic radiation in the visible and near-visible spectrum — encompassing absorption, reflection, transmission, refraction, emission, and scattering. These properties arise from the electronic structure of materials: how electrons in atoms, molecules, or band structures respond to incident photons. The field bridges fundamental physics (Maxwell's equations, quantum electrodynamics) with applied materials science (photonic devices, optical coatings, display technologies).

At the nanoscale, optical properties become dramatically tunable. Quantum confinement in semiconductor nanocrystals shifts emission wavelengths with particle size. Plasmonic nanoparticles exhibit localized surface plasmon resonances that concentrate electromagnetic fields far below the diffraction limit. Photonic crystals engineer the optical band gap to create materials that forbid certain wavelengths of light. These engineered optical behaviors underpin technologies from fiber-optic communications to solar cells to biomedical imaging.

## Key Properties

- **Band gap dependence**: A material's optical absorption edge is determined by its electronic band gap — direct band gap semiconductors (GaAs, CdSe) emit light efficiently; indirect band gap materials (Si) require phonon assistance
- **Refractive index**: Governs light speed in a medium and determines reflection, refraction, and waveguiding behavior; can be engineered via material composition and nanostructuring
- **Photoluminescence**: Emission of light after photon absorption — quantum yield, emission wavelength, and Stokes shift characterize a material's luminescent performance
- **Plasmonic response**: Noble metal nanoparticles (Au, Ag) exhibit collective electron oscillations that create intense localized electromagnetic fields, enabling sub-wavelength optics
- **Nonlinear optical effects**: At high intensities, materials exhibit second-harmonic generation, Kerr effect, and multi-photon absorption — enabling frequency conversion and ultrafast switching

## Examples

- **Photonic nanocavities**: Graphite polytype switching creates nanocavities with tunable optical resonances, enabling on-chip light manipulation
- **Quantum dots**: CdSe/ZnS core-shell nanocrystals with size-tunable emission from blue (2nm) to red (6nm), used in displays and biological imaging
- **Metamaterials**: Engineered sub-wavelength structures that achieve negative refractive index, enabling superlensing and cloaking at specific frequencies
- **Perovskite solar cells**: Methylammonium lead halide perovskites combine strong optical absorption with long carrier diffusion lengths, achieving over 26% photovoltaic efficiency
