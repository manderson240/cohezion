# Licensing

Cohezion is **dual-licensed**. You may use it under **either**:

1. the open-source **GNU AGPL-3.0** (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)), **or**
2. a **commercial license** purchased from the copyright holder.

You only need a commercial license if the AGPL's obligations don't work for your use
case. If you're doing research, evaluation, or building other open-source (AGPL-compatible)
software, the AGPL is free and sufficient.

## What the AGPL-3.0 requires

The AGPL is a strong copyleft license. In plain terms:

- You may **use, modify, and self-host** Cohezion for free.
- If you **distribute** Cohezion (or a derivative), you must provide the complete
  corresponding source under the AGPL.
- **Network use counts.** If you run a **modified** version of Cohezion as a service that
  others interact with over a network, you must offer those users the modified source code
  under the AGPL (§13). This is the key difference from plain GPL.

If you can comply with those terms, you never need to talk to us.

## When you need a commercial license

A commercial license removes the AGPL's source-disclosure obligations. Typical reasons to
buy one:

- You want to embed Cohezion in a **closed-source product**.
- You want to offer a **hosted/SaaS service** built on a modified Cohezion **without**
  publishing your modifications.
- Your company **policy or customers prohibit AGPL** dependencies.
- You want a warranty, indemnification, or support terms the AGPL does not provide.

Commercial licenses can be structured as a one-time license, a subscription, or
usage-based terms — we're happy to fit the arrangement to how you're using it.

## How to obtain a commercial license

Open an issue tagged `licensing`, or email **manderson240@gmail.com** with a short
description of your intended use. We'll follow up with terms.

## Contributions

Contributions are accepted under the
[Contributor License Agreement](CONTRIBUTOR_LICENSE_AGREEMENT.md). The CLA assigns the
project the rights it needs to offer Cohezion under **both** the AGPL and commercial
licenses. By submitting a pull request you agree to its terms. This is what keeps the
dual-license model possible — without it, third-party contributions could not be included
in the commercially licensed builds.
