## Abstract

A copper superatom nanocluster (Cu45) achieves 81.8% selectivity in converting CO2 to ethylene and other multi-carbon products. This well-defined catalyst maintains exceptional stability across extreme chemical conditions and outperforms standard copper nanoparticles.

## Key Findings

- Cu45 superatom achieves 81.8% Faradaic efficiency for multi-carbon products with 58% selectivity for ethylene, surpassing all known copper cluster catalysts
- The superatom demonstrates extraordinary stability under thermal, oxidative, reductive, acidic, and basic treatments due to its superatomic electronic configuration
- Cu45 outperformed commercial copper nanoparticles by 17% under identical conditions, rivaling the best copper-based catalysts in literature
- This represents the first well-defined copper superatom electrocatalyst applied to CO2-to-ethylene electrocatalysis
