## Abstract

NASA has assembled a formal anomaly review board to investigate the loss of contact with the MAVEN Mars orbiter, which ceased communications on December 6, 2025. The spacecraft had been tumbling with indications of possible trajectory changes, representing significant loss of both Mars atmospheric science capabilities and critical telecommunications relay support for surface rovers.

## Key Findings

- MAVEN spacecraft lost contact on December 6, 2025, with final telemetry indicating tumbling and possible trajectory change
- Solar conjunction (Sun between Earth and Mars) complicated recovery attempts and communication restoration efforts
- MAVEN served dual critical roles: Mars atmospheric science mission and telecommunications relay for Curiosity and Perseverance rovers
- Formal anomaly review board assembly indicates NASA considers situation serious and warrants comprehensive investigation
- Loss impacts both scientific understanding of Mars atmosphere and ongoing rover mission operations

# NASA MAVEN Anomaly Review

## Summary

NASA is assembling a formal anomaly review board to investigate the loss of contact with the MAVEN Mars orbiter, last heard from on December 6, 2025. The spacecraft was tumbling and had possibly changed trajectory. MAVEN served dual roles as a science mission studying Mars atmosphere and as a telecommunications relay for Curiosity and Perseverance rovers.

## Key Points

- Final data indicated spacecraft tumbling with possible trajectory change.
- Solar conjunction (Sun between Earth and Mars) complicated recovery attempts.
- Loss of MAVEN impacts both Mars atmospheric science and rover communications.
- Assembly of formal review board signals NASA considers the situation serious.
