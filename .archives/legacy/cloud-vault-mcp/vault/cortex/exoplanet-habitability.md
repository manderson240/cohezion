## Definition

Assessment of life-supporting conditions on distant planets, primarily through liquid water presence, stable climates, and Earth-like characteristics. Recent research (2024-2025) expanded definitions beyond liquid water to include ionic liquids on water-depleted worlds, with the TRAPPIST-1 system (discovered by Gillon et al., 2017) providing seven rocky planets as key study targets.

## Key Properties

- Seven physical parameters including radius, density, orbital eccentricity determine habitability scoring
- Atmospheric spectroscopy revealing reduced CO2 indicates active water cycling and potential biospheres
- Extended definitions now include ionic liquids on warm, thin-atmosphere worlds
- New metrics using surface temperature, precipitation, and evaporation patterns
- JWST enabling direct atmospheric characterization of nearby exoplanets

## Examples

- TRAPPIST-1e: Earth-sized exoplanet (~70% Earth mass) in the habitable zone with potential secondary atmosphere
- TRAPPIST-1 outer planets (f, g): sufficient water content and potential secondary atmosphere formation
