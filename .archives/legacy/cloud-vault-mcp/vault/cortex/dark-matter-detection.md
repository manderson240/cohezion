## Definition

Techniques to identify dark matter (~85% of all matter), including axion searches, weak gravitational lensing, and fast radio burst observations. The axion hypothesis (Peccei & Quinn, 1977; named by Frank Wilczek after a laundry detergent) proposes a particle that solves the strong CP problem and was recognized as a dark matter candidate in 1983.

## Key Properties

- Axion mass range 0.2-3 meV identified as sweet spot with detectable microlensing events
- Weak lensing surveys (Vera Rubin, Euclid, Roman) measure matter power spectrum revealing axion non-linearities
- Gravitationally lensed FRBs enable differential birefringence measurements as axion probes
- Direct detection experiments (XENON, LUX) report null results for WIMPs, driving focus to axion candidates
- Axion haloscope experiments using quantum-limited amplifiers excluding significant mass parameter ranges

## Examples

- Axion miniclusters: dense asteroid-sized clumps producing detectable gravitational lensing variations
- Lensed quasar HS 0810+2554: wavy critical curves better explained by axion quantum interference than WIMPs
