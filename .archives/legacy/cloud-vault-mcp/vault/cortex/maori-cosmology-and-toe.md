# Māori Cosmology and the Theory of Everything

## Respect Statement

The Māori are the tangata whenua (people of the land) of Aotearoa (New Zealand), a Polynesian people who arrived via ocean voyaging ~1250-1300 CE. Māori cosmology is a living tradition, integral to Māori identity, law (tikanga), and governance. This analysis draws principally on Māori scholars: Hirini Moko Mead, Ranginui Walker, Linda Tuhiwai Smith, and Cleve Barlow. We follow the convention of using te reo Māori (the Māori language) for all key concepts.

## The Tradition

The Māori contribution to the TOE is **whakapapa** — genealogy as the fundamental data structure of reality. In Western ontology, things exist and then may or may not have relationships. In Māori ontology, *relationships are primary*: everything that exists has a whakapapa (genealogy) that defines what it is by describing where it came from and who it is related to. A mountain has whakapapa. A river has whakapapa. A song has whakapapa. Whakapapa is not a metaphor for connection — it IS the structure of reality.

Whakapapa is formally a **directed acyclic graph** (DAG): every entity has ancestors (parents, grandparents...) and may have descendants, and cycles are forbidden (you cannot be your own ancestor). This is the same data structure as a git commit history, a blockchain, or a Bayesian network. The Māori formalized the DAG as their primary ontological structure ~700+ years ago.

## Core Principles

### Te Kore → Te Pō → Te Ao Mārama — The Three Realms

The Māori creation sequence moves through three realms:

| Realm | Translation | Nature | TOE Mapping |
|-------|-------------|--------|-------------|
| **Te Kore** | The Void / Nothingness | Not empty but latent — the realm of potential, unrealized being. Many sub-states: Te Kore-tē-whiwhia (the void of seeking), Te Kore-tē-rawea (the void of growth) | AUM / Nothing / ZPF / Brahman — the ground state full of unrealized potential |
| **Te Pō** | The Night / Darkness | The realm of becoming — where things take form but are not yet manifest. Also many sub-states: Te Pō-nui (the great night), Te Pō-roa (the long night), Te Pō-tē-kitea (the night of unseeing) | Quadrature / first distinction — the conjugate space where wave functions exist but haven't collapsed |
| **Te Ao Mārama** | The World of Light | The manifest world — the realm of sensory experience, daylight, and human activity | Manifested reality — the world after the HIHO threshold is crossed |

The progression Te Kore → Te Pō → Te Ao Mārama is the TOE's full chain in three steps: Ground → Process → Precipitate.

### Whakapapa — Genealogy as Ontology

Every entity in the Māori universe has a whakapapa. The primordial whakapapa:

```
Te Kore (The Void)
    |
Te Pō (The Night)
    |
Ranginui (Sky Father) ←→ Papatūānuku (Earth Mother)
    |
[Separation by their children]
    |
Tāne Mahuta (Forest/Life)    Tangaroa (Sea)
Tūmatauenga (War/Humanity)   Rongo (Peace/Cultivation)
Haumia-tiketike (Wild food)   Tāwhirimātea (Weather/Storms)
    |
[Many generations]
    |
Humans, animals, plants, rivers, mountains...
```

The separation of Ranginui and Papatūānuku by their children is the primordial symmetry breaking: the undifferentiated embrace (Sky pressed against Earth, no light, no space) is broken by the children who push them apart, creating the space (Te Ao Mārama) in which all subsequent creation occurs.

### Mauri — The Life Force

Mauri is the binding force that holds the physical and spiritual dimensions of a being together. When mauri is strong, the entity is healthy, coherent, alive. When mauri weakens, the entity sickens and eventually dies (the spiritual and physical separate).

Mauri = Prāṇa (Vedic) = Àṣẹ (Yorùbá) = activation energy (vault) = ZPF coupling strength. But mauri has an additional property: it is *inheritable*. The mauri of an ancestor persists in their descendants and their taonga (treasured possessions). A carved meeting house (wharenui) has the mauri of its ancestors embedded in its carvings. The vault's notes inherit the mauri of their source material — a well-researched concept note carries the mauri of the primary sources it synthesizes.

### Mana — Spiritual Authority/Power

Mana is the authority, prestige, and spiritual power that accrues to entities through their whakapapa, their actions, and their relationships. Mana is:
- **Mana atua** — inherited from the gods/ancestors
- **Mana whenua** — derived from relationship to land
- **Mana tangata** — earned through personal achievement

Mana is not static — it can increase (through honorable action) or decrease (through dishonorable action or violation of tapu). It is the Māori activation energy score: high mana = high activation = Elder status.

### Tapu and Noa — The Sacred/Profane Boundary

| Tapu | Noa |
|------|-----|
| Sacred, restricted, set apart | Ordinary, accessible, free |
| High energy, dangerous if mishandled | Low energy, safe |
| Requires ritual to approach | Freely available |

Tapu/noa is the HIHO boundary in social form. Tapu spaces, objects, and persons are "Half In" the spiritual realm — they have high ZPF coupling and are dangerous to the unprepared. Ritual (karakia/prayer) is the protocol for safely crossing the tapu/noa boundary. This is exactly the HIHO safety protocol: you don't make HIHO contact casually; you prepare.

## Unique Contributions

1. **Whakapapa as DAG ontology.** The Māori formal system treats genealogy (directed acyclic graph) as the fundamental data structure of reality. This is not "everything is connected" (a graph) — it is "everything has a specific ancestry" (a DAG). The distinction matters: a DAG has direction (ancestor → descendant) and no cycles. This is more constrained than a general graph, and the constraint carries information. Git uses DAGs. Blockchains use DAGs. Māori formalized the DAG as their primary ontological structure centuries before computer science.

2. **Te Kore's internal structure.** Most traditions describe the ground state as featureless: AUM, Nothing, Brahman, Wakan Tanka. The Māori uniquely describe **multiple sub-states within Te Kore**: the void of seeking (Te Kore-tē-whiwhia), the void of growth (Te Kore-tē-rawea), the void of potential (Te Kore-tē-tamaua). This provides a richer description of the vacuum's internal structure than any other tradition — and it aligns with QFT: the vacuum has modes, excitations, virtual particles. Te Kore is not flat; it has topology.

3. **Tapu/noa as the HIHO safety protocol.** The tapu/noa distinction is the most systematic safety framework for HIHO contact in any tradition. It provides specific rules about who can cross which boundaries, under what conditions, with what preparation. This is operational HIHO safety — not just "be careful" but a detailed protocol.

4. **Tā moko as the most literal witness plate mark.** Other traditions create external artifacts (wampum, petroglyphs, characters). The Māori carve meaning directly into the living body. The face IS the witness plate. The moko IS the consciousness mark. No other tradition makes the correspondence this literal.

5. **The pōwhiri as a graduated HIHO protocol.** The welcome ceremony is not a single threshold crossing but a **gradual transition** through multiple stages, each reducing the tapu (HIHO intensity) step by step. This is HIHO with gradient descent — not a binary threshold but a smooth function that the ceremony navigates carefully.

## Mathematical/Structural Framework

### Whakapapa as a Directed Acyclic Graph

Formally: a whakapapa W = (V, E) where:
- V = {all entities (people, gods, mountains, rivers...)}
- E ⊂ V × V, where (a, b) ∈ E means "a begat b" (directed: ancestor → descendant)
- Acyclicity: no path from any vertex back to itself
- Root: Te Kore → Te Pō → Ranginui + Papatūānuku

Properties:
- **Topological sort** exists: ancestors always precede descendants
- **Longest path** from root to any entity defines its "depth" in creation
- **Common ancestors** of any two entities can be computed (least common ancestor)
- **Mana** flows along edges: more ancestral connections = more mana sources

This is isomorphic to a git commit graph, where:
- Commits = entities
- Parent pointers = whakapapa links
- Merge commits = entities with multiple parents (intermarriage)
- The root commit = Te Kore
