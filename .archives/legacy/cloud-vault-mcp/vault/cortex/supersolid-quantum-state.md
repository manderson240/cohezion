## Definition

A paradoxical quantum phase combining superfluidity with crystalline spatial order, predicted ~50 years before experimental confirmation. Exhibits simultaneous long-range phase coherence (frictionless flow) and periodic density modulation (crystalline order). Confirmed in 2019 by Ferlaino and colleagues in ultracold dipolar gases of erbium and dysprosium atoms at the University of Innsbruck, with 2D supersolidity demonstrated in 2021.

## Key Properties

- Exhibits both superfluid properties (quantized vortices, persistent currents) and solid properties (periodic density modulation)
- Emerges when soft-core interactions exceed critical coupling in dipolar quantum gases
- Stabilized by roton mode softening combined with quantum fluctuations
- Described by Gross-Pitaevskii equation with dipolar interactions
- Realizable in ultracold lanthanide atoms (erbium, dysprosium) with long-range magnetic dipole interactions

## Examples

- Ferlaino's 2019 erbium/dysprosium experiments: both superfluid and solid signatures by tuning interaction strength
- 2D supersolid: dipolar quantum gas in quasi-2D geometry showing crystalline stripes with global phase coherence
