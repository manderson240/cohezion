## Definition

Matsumoto's HIHO synthesis proposes that hydrogen isotopes loaded into metallic lattices (particularly palladium and nickel) can form coherent, high-density clusters called "itons." When electromagnetic conditions at lattice defect sites cross a stability threshold, these clusters undergo rapid compression leading to nuclear transmutation products -- including excess heat, neutron emission, and elemental transmutation -- without the extreme temperatures normally required for fusion.

## Key Properties

- **Itonic cluster formation** -- Hydrogen isotopes aggregate at lattice defect sites into coherent clusters with anomalous density
- **Coulomb violation threshold** -- At sufficient cluster density, electromagnetic screening effects allow nuclear proximity below the classical Coulomb barrier
- **Electromagnetic force dominance** -- In the condensed matter regime, electromagnetic interactions dominate over gravitational and weak forces, enabling confinement mechanisms unavailable in free space
- **Coherence stability** -- Clusters maintain coherence only within specific ranges of hydrogen loading ratio, temperature, and electromagnetic field strength
- **Transmutation products** -- Predicted and observed products include helium-4, tritium, and heavier elements not present in initial materials
