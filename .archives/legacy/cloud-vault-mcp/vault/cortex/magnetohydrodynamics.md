# Magnetohydrodynamics

## Definition

Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) is the study of the dynamics of electrically conducting fluids in the presence of magnetic fields. It unifies classical fluid mechanics (Navier-Stokes equations) with Maxwell's electrodynamics, treating the fluid and the magnetic field as a coupled system. MHD applies to plasmas, liquid metals, saltwater, and electrolytes — any medium with free charge carriers that can support electrical currents. The fundamental insight is that a moving conductor generates currents which produce magnetic forces that modify the flow — the fluid and the field are inseparable.

MHD was founded by Hannes Alfvén (Nobel Prize 1970), who showed that magnetic field lines can be "frozen into" perfectly conducting fluids and that transverse waves (Alfvén waves) propagate along field lines.

## Key Properties

### Frozen-in Flux (Alfvén's Theorem)

In the limit of infinite electrical conductivity (η → 0), magnetic flux through any surface co-moving with the fluid is conserved:

> d/dt [integral_S B . dA] = 0

Equivalently, magnetic field lines move with the fluid — they are "frozen in." This is the MHD analogue of Kelvin's circulation theorem for ideal fluids. The physical consequence: the topology of the magnetic field evolves only as the fluid moves; field lines cannot reconnect in ideal MHD.

### Magnetic Reynolds Number

The competition between advection (freezing-in) and diffusion (reconnection) is quantified by:

> Rm = v * L / η

where v is a characteristic velocity, L is a characteristic length scale, and η = 1/(μ₀σ) is the magnetic diffusivity (σ = electrical conductivity). For Rm >> 1: frozen-in approximation holds. For Rm << 1: diffusion dominates, fields rapidly diffuse through the fluid. Stellar interiors: Rm ~ 10⁸. Laboratory plasmas: Rm ~ 10⁻³ to 10³. Earth's core: Rm ~ 300.

### Alfvén Velocity

The speed of transverse MHD waves propagating along magnetic field lines:

> v_A = B / sqrt(μ₀ * ρ)

For the solar corona (B ~ 10⁻³ T, ρ ~ 10⁻¹³ kg/m³): v_A ~ 10³ km/s. In the Earth's liquid outer core (B ~ 4×10⁻³ T, ρ ~ 10⁴ kg/m³): v_A ~ 4 mm/s. Alfvén waves carry energy and angular momentum along field lines — they are the communication channel by which distant field regions influence each other.

## Mathematical Framework

### The MHD Equations

The ideal MHD equations (η = 0, ν = 0):

**Momentum (force balance):**
> ρ (∂v/∂t + (v·∇)v) = -∇p + J×B + ρg

where J×B is the Lorentz force on the current-carrying fluid.

**Induction equation (field evolution):**
> ∂B/∂t = ∇×(v×B) + η∇²B

The first term is advection (frozen-in transport); the second is resistive diffusion.

**Continuity (mass conservation):**
> ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0

**Energy equation:**
> ρ (∂ε/∂t + v·∇ε) = -p∇·v + η|J|² + heating - cooling

**Divergence constraint:**
> ∇·B = 0

**Ampère's law (MHD limit — displacement current neglected):**
> J = (1/μ₀) ∇×B

### Resistive MHD

Including finite resistivity η:
> ∂B/∂t = ∇×(v×B) + η∇²B

The resistive term η∇²B allows field topology to change — magnetic reconnection occurs. The Sweet-Parker reconnection rate (1958):
> v_rec = v_A / sqrt(Rm)

This is too slow to explain observed reconnection in solar flares. Fast reconnection (Petschek 1964) introduces localized diffusion regions with outflow jets:
> v_rec ~ v_A / log(Rm)

### Magnetostatics and Force-Free Fields

When magnetic pressure dominates (β << 1), the fluid is force-free:
> J × B = 0 → ∇×B = α(x)B

where α is the force-free parameter. Linear force-free fields (α = const) satisfy:
> ∇²B + α²B = 0

the Beltrami equation. Woltjer's theorem: the minimum energy state of an isolated conducting volume is a linear force-free field, and the magnetic helicity H = ∫ A·B d³x is conserved.

### MHD Waves

MHD supports three distinct wave modes:
1. **Alfvén wave:** Transverse, non-compressive, propagates along B at v_A
2. **Fast magnetosonic wave:** Compressive, propagates in all directions, speed > v_A
3. **Slow magnetosonic wave:** Compressive, propagates near field-aligned direction, speed < v_A

In the solar wind, these three modes carry energy from the corona to the heliosphere.

### The Dynamo Problem

Earth's geomagnetic field is generated by convective motion of liquid iron in the outer core — a self-sustaining MHD dynamo. The kinematic dynamo condition:
> ∂B/∂t = ∇×(v×B) + η∇²B

requires that the flow v amplifies and sustains B against ohmic dissipation. The anti-dynamo theorems (Cowling 1934) prove that purely axisymmetric flows cannot maintain a dynamo — three-dimensionality is essential. Modern geodynamo simulations (Glatzmaier & Roberts 1995) reproduce observed field morphology and polarity reversals.

### Magnetorotational Instability (MRI)

In accretion disks, a weak magnetic field destabilizes differentially rotating conducting fluid:

For a fluid with angular velocity Ω(r), a weak vertical field B_z is unstable when:
> d(Ω²)/d(r) < 0 and k²v_A² < -d(Ω²)/d(ln r)

where k is the wavenumber of the perturbation. The fastest-growing MRI mode has:
> k²v_A² = (15/16) Ω²  with  γ_max = (3/4) Ω

The MRI (Balbus & Hawley 1991) explains anomalous angular momentum transport in accretion disks — turbulent viscosity 10⁸× molecular viscosity — resolving a decades-old puzzle.

## Examples

- **Solar corona:** MHD explains the million-degree corona, solar wind acceleration, flares, CMEs. The Parker Solar Probe samples in-situ MHD turbulence at 0.04 AU.
- **Tokamak fusion:** ITER confines deuterium-tritium plasma via magnetic geometry (torus) stabilized against MHD instabilities (kink, ballooning modes).
- **Stellar magnetic activity:** Differential rotation + convection → α-Ω dynamo → star spots, flares, cycle periodicity.
- **Liquid metal flows:** Mercury in pipes exposed to strong fields develops Hartmann boundary layers of thickness δ_Ha = L/sqrt(Ha) where Ha = BL·sqrt(σ/ρν) is the Hartmann number.
- **Galaxy formation:** Magnetic fields in the ISM (~ 10⁻¹⁰ T) contribute pressure comparable to thermal pressure, influencing star formation rates.
