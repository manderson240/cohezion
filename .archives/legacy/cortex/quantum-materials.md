# Quantum Materials

Materials exhibiting quantum mechanical effects at macroscopic scales — including superconductors, topological insulators, strongly correlated electron systems, and quantum spin liquids. These materials bridge fundamental quantum mechanics and practical technology, with applications ranging from MRI machines to quantum computing hardware.

## Definition

Quantum materials are condensed matter systems whose macroscopic properties are governed by quantum mechanical phenomena that cannot be explained by classical physics. The key distinguishing feature is that quantum coherence, entanglement, or topological order persists at scales visible to bulk measurement, rather than being washed out by thermal fluctuations.

## Key Classes

### Superconductors

Materials with zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature. Conventional superconductors are explained by BCS theory (Cooper pair formation via phonon-mediated attraction). High-temperature cuprate superconductors (Tc up to 135 K) involve magnetic spin fluctuation pairing mechanisms. See [[superconductivity]] for details.

### Topological Insulators

Materials that are electrically insulating in their bulk but conduct on their surfaces or edges via topologically protected states. The surface conduction is robust against disorder due to spin-momentum locking and topological invariants (Z2 indices, Chern numbers). See [[topological-insulators]] for details.

### Strongly Correlated Systems

Materials where electron-electron interactions dominate over kinetic energy, producing emergent phenomena: Mott insulators, heavy fermion behavior, colossal magnetoresistance, and unconventional superconductivity. These systems defy single-particle band theory descriptions.

### Quantum Spin Liquids

Magnetic materials where quantum fluctuations prevent magnetic ordering even at absolute zero, maintaining a highly entangled quantum ground state. Candidates include herbertsmithite and certain kagome lattice materials.

## Key Properties

- **Emergent behavior:** Macroscopic quantum phenomena emerge from collective interactions of many particles, not from single-particle physics
- **Phase transitions:** Quantum materials exhibit quantum phase transitions driven by parameters other than temperature (pressure, magnetic field, doping)
- **Topological protection:** Some quantum properties are protected by topology and symmetry, making them robust against perturbations — a key feature for quantum computing applications

## Applications

- **Medical imaging:** MRI machines rely on superconducting magnets
- **Quantum computing:** Topological qubits from Majorana fermions in topological superconductors; superconducting transmon qubits
- **Metrology:** Quantum Hall resistance standards (exact quantized values)
- **Energy:** High-temperature superconductor cables for lossless power transmission; thermoelectric generators from topological materials
