# Cosmology

## Definition

Cosmology is the branch of astrophysics concerned with the origin, large-scale structure, and evolution of the universe as a whole. It combines observational data from telescopes, satellites, and particle detectors with theoretical frameworks from general relativity and quantum field theory to answer fundamental questions about the universe's age, composition, geometry, and fate.

## Key Properties

- **Standard Model (Lambda-CDM):** The Lambda cold dark matter model describes the universe with just six parameters: ordinary matter (~5%), cold dark matter (~27%), dark energy (~68%), the Hubble constant, the scalar spectral index, and the optical depth to reionization. It accounts for the cosmic microwave background, large-scale structure, element abundances, and accelerating expansion.
- **Precision cosmology:** Missions such as Planck, WMAP, DESI, and Euclid have constrained cosmological parameters to sub-percent uncertainty, transforming cosmology from order-of-magnitude estimates into a precision science.
- **Hubble tension:** The locally measured expansion rate (via Cepheids and supernovae) disagrees with the CMB-inferred value at >5 sigma, hinting at possible new physics beyond Lambda-CDM.
- **Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO):** Sound waves frozen into the matter distribution at recombination provide a cosmic standard ruler used by surveys like DESI to measure expansion history.
- **Dark energy equation of state:** Whether dark energy is a true cosmological constant or evolves over time is one of the central open questions; DESI preliminary data suggest possible evolution at ~3.9 sigma tension with a constant.

## Examples

- The Planck 2018 data release provided the most precise full-sky CMB map, establishing the six-parameter Lambda-CDM as the standard cosmological model.
- DESI's baryon acoustic oscillation measurements detected neutrino-induced suppression of small-scale cosmic structure, advancing precision constraints on neutrino mass from cosmological observations.
- The discovery of accelerating expansion via Type Ia supernovae (Riess et al. 1998, Perlmutter et al. 1999) established dark energy as a dominant component of the universe.
