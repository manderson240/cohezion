# AI for Good

The application of artificial intelligence technologies to address pressing social, environmental, and humanitarian challenges. AI for Good encompasses using ML for healthcare diagnostics, climate modeling, biodiversity monitoring, disaster response, accessibility, and education -- directing AI capabilities toward measurable positive impact rather than purely commercial objectives.

## Definition

AI for Good represents the intentional deployment of machine learning and AI systems to solve problems that benefit society at large, particularly underserved populations and vulnerable ecosystems. The field spans applications in global health (disease diagnosis and drug discovery), environmental sustainability (climate prediction, deforestation monitoring), humanitarian action (crisis mapping, refugee assistance), and accessibility (assistive technologies for people with disabilities). It requires careful attention to ethical considerations, equitable access, and avoiding the reinforcement of existing biases.

## Key Properties

- **Impact measurement** -- AI for Good projects require rigorous measurement of real-world outcomes, not just model accuracy, to demonstrate genuine social benefit
- **Data challenges** -- Social good applications often face data scarcity, label noise, and distribution shift in under-resourced contexts where high-quality labeled datasets are unavailable
- **Equity and access** -- Solutions must work for the intended beneficiaries, including low-resource settings with limited connectivity, older devices, and non-English languages
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration** -- Effective deployment requires partnership between ML researchers, domain experts (clinicians, ecologists, social workers), and affected communities
- **Dual-use awareness** -- Technologies developed for social good (surveillance for conservation, facial recognition for accessibility) can be repurposed for harmful applications

## Examples

- **Medical imaging in low-resource settings** -- Deep learning models diagnosing diabetic retinopathy, tuberculosis, and malaria from smartphone-captured images in rural clinics
- **Climate and biodiversity monitoring** -- Satellite image classification for deforestation tracking, acoustic monitoring for species identification, and ocean temperature prediction
- **MELLODDY consortium** -- Federated learning enabling pharmaceutical companies to collaboratively train drug discovery models without sharing proprietary data
