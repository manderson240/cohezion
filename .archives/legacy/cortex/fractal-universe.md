## Definition

Fractal cosmology is the hypothesis that the universe exhibits self-similar structure across scales — that the patterns observed at smaller scales (galaxy clusters, filaments, voids) are repeated at progressively larger scales, analogous to mathematical fractals. First proposed in the context of galaxy clustering by Benoit Mandelbrot in the 1970s and formalized by Luciano Pietronero in the 1980s, the idea challenges the standard Cosmological Principle's assumption of large-scale homogeneity.

The current scientific consensus, informed by surveys such as SDSS and DESI, is nuanced: galaxy distributions do exhibit fractal-like clustering with a fractal dimension near 2 on scales up to approximately 70-100 Megaparsecs per h (Mpc/h), but transition to statistical homogeneity (fractal dimension 3) beyond that threshold. Scrimgeour et al. (2012) provided definitive evidence of this homogeneity transition. The universe is not a pure fractal, but fractal geometry accurately describes its intermediate-scale structure.

## Key Properties

- **Scale-dependent fractal dimension**: The fractal dimension of galaxy distribution is approximately 2 on scales below 20 Mpc/h, transitioning to 3 (homogeneous) above 70-100 Mpc/h
- **Self-similarity in the cosmic web**: Filaments, walls, and voids form a web-like structure that resembles fractal patterns at intermediate scales
- **Connection to inflation**: Chaotic eternal inflation naturally produces hierarchical matter distributions with fractal properties through quantum fluctuations generating overdense and underdense regions
- **Implications for dark energy**: Some fractal models propose that a varying gravitational constant G with fractal scaling could explain accelerated expansion without requiring a cosmological constant, though this remains speculative
- **Quantum gravity connection**: In asymptotic safety quantum gravity, spacetime reduces to a spectral dimension of 2 on sub-Planckian scales, suggesting fractal-like geometry at the smallest scales

## Examples

- **Cosmic web structure**: The distribution of galaxies forms filaments surrounding voids in a pattern that is visually self-similar across scales from ~1 Mpc to ~100 Mpc
- **Large-scale galaxy surveys**: The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) measured the two-point correlation function of luminous red galaxies, finding power-law scaling (a fractal signature) up to intermediate scales
- **N-body simulations**: Cosmological N-body codes (e.g., Gadget, HACC) reproduce fractal-like clustering naturally when gravitational collapse is simulated from primordial density perturbations
