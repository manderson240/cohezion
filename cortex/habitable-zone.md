# Habitable Zone

## Definition

The habitable zone (HZ, also called the Goldilocks zone) is the region around a star where conditions permit liquid water to exist on a planet's surface, given sufficient atmospheric pressure. First formalized by Kasting et al. (1993), the HZ is defined primarily by the stellar luminosity and the planet's orbital distance, though actual habitability depends on many additional factors including atmospheric composition, magnetic field strength, tidal effects, and geological activity.

The HZ is the most widely used criterion for identifying potentially life-bearing exoplanets, but it is increasingly recognized as a **necessary but not sufficient** condition. A planet in the HZ may still be uninhabitable (e.g., if it lacks an atmosphere or magnetic field), while a planet outside the HZ may harbor liquid water subsurface (e.g., Europa, Enceladus).

## Boundaries

### Conservative Habitable Zone (Kasting et al. 1993, updated Kopparapu et al. 2013)

| Boundary | Mechanism | Solar System Distance |
|----------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Inner edge** (Runaway greenhouse) | Water vapor feedback: increased radiation → more evaporation → more greenhouse → ocean loss | ~0.95 AU |
| **Inner edge** (Moist greenhouse) | Water vapor reaches stratosphere → photodissociation → hydrogen escape → irreversible water loss | ~0.99 AU |
| **Outer edge** (Maximum greenhouse) | CO2 condensation limits warming; Rayleigh scattering increases albedo | ~1.67 AU |
| **Outer edge** (First CO2 condensation) | CO2 begins condensing as clouds, reducing greenhouse warming | ~1.70 AU |

### Extended Habitable Zone Concepts

| Extension | Mechanism | Implications |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Subsurface HZ** | Tidal heating, radiogenic heating maintain liquid water beneath ice | Europa, Enceladus — life without starlight |
| **Terminator HZ** | Tidally locked planets with habitable twilight zone | Expands M-dwarf HZ candidates (see [[tidally-locked-exoplanet-habitability]]) |
| **Galactic HZ** | Distance from galactic center balances metallicity (needed for rocky planets) against radiation/supernova frequency | Constrains where habitable systems can exist |

## Key Properties

- **Inner boundary**: Set by the runaway greenhouse effect, where increased stellar radiation causes water vapor feedback that evaporates all surface water
- **Outer boundary**: Set by the maximum greenhouse effect, beyond which CO2 condensation prevents sufficient warming for liquid water
- **Stellar type dependence**: M-dwarf stars (70% of all stars) have narrow, close-in HZs (~0.1-0.4 AU); G-type stars (like the Sun) have wider zones (~0.95-1.7 AU); A-type stars have HZs at 2-5+ AU
- **Tidal locking**: Planets in the HZs of M-dwarfs are almost always tidally locked, creating permanent day and night hemispheres — but atmospheric circulation may redistribute heat
- **Evolving boundaries**: As stars age and brighten, the HZ migrates outward. Earth will leave the Sun's HZ in ~1.5-2.5 Gyr as the Sun's luminosity increases
- **Atmospheric dependence**: The HZ width depends critically on atmospheric composition — a thick CO2 atmosphere extends the outer edge; hydrogen-helium atmospheres could maintain surface water at much greater distances
- **Magnetic field requirement**: Without a magnetic field, stellar wind erosion strips atmospheres on Gyr timescales, particularly for close-in planets around active M-dwarfs (see [[super-earth-magnetic-protection-magma]])

## Examples

- **Earth** orbits within the Sun's HZ at approximately 1 AU — the reference case
- **Venus** (~0.72 AU) is inside the inner edge; likely had oceans that were lost via a runaway greenhouse
- **Mars** (~1.52 AU) is near the outer edge; thin atmosphere cannot maintain surface liquid water today, but geological evidence suggests past surface water
- **TRAPPIST-1 e, f, g**: Three Earth-sized planets in the HZ of an M-dwarf, with JWST atmospheric characterization underway
- Tidally locked exoplanets may maintain habitability at the terminator between day and night sides (see [[tidally-locked-exoplanet-habitability]])
- Recent research challenges traditional HZ definitions by considering subsurface oceans and atmospheric dynamics (see [[rethinking-exoplanet-habitability]])
