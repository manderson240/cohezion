# Robust AI

The design of AI systems that maintain correct behavior under adversarial attacks, distribution shift, noisy inputs, and unexpected operating conditions. Robust AI encompasses adversarial machine learning (attack and defense), uncertainty quantification (Bayesian methods, conformal prediction), out-of-distribution detection, formal verification, and failure mode engineering to ensure AI systems are reliable and safe in production.

## Definition

Robust AI addresses the gap between controlled laboratory performance and real-world reliability. Models that achieve high accuracy on test sets can fail catastrophically when faced with adversarial perturbations, novel input distributions, or corrupted data. Robustness research develops both attacks (to understand vulnerabilities) and defenses (to mitigate them), alongside principled methods for quantifying model uncertainty and detecting when inputs fall outside the training distribution.

## Key Properties

- **Adversarial vulnerability** -- Small, imperceptible perturbations to inputs can cause confident misclassification (FGSM, PGD, C&W attacks demonstrate this across modalities)
- **Distribution shift** -- Performance degrades when deployment data differs from training data due to temporal drift, geographic variation, or subpopulation imbalance
- **Uncertainty quantification** -- Bayesian neural networks, Monte Carlo dropout, ensemble methods, and conformal prediction provide calibrated confidence estimates that distinguish known-unknowns from unknown-unknowns
- **Certified defense** -- Provable robustness guarantees (randomized smoothing, Lipschitz constraints) that bound worst-case behavior within specified perturbation radii
- **Graceful degradation** -- Systems that fail safely (refuse prediction, escalate to human, reduce confidence) rather than producing silently wrong outputs

## Examples

- **Adversarial training** -- Augmenting training data with adversarial examples generated by PGD to improve model resilience against gradient-based attacks
- **Out-of-distribution detection** -- Using energy-based scores or Mahalanobis distance to flag inputs that lie far from the training distribution, triggering human review
- **Formal verification of neural networks** -- Proving that a network's classification is invariant within an L-infinity ball around each test input, using abstract interpretation or SMT solvers
- **Red team testing** -- Systematic adversarial evaluation of deployed AI systems (language models, image classifiers, autonomous driving) to discover failure modes before release
