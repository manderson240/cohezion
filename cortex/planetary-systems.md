## Definition

Planetary systems are gravitationally bound collections of non-stellar bodies orbiting one or more stars, including planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets. Our Solar System is one example, but thousands of exoplanetary systems have been confirmed since the first discoveries in the 1990s. Planetary systems exhibit enormous diversity in architecture, from tightly packed systems of super-Earths to widely spaced gas giant configurations.

## Key Properties

- **Orbital dynamics**: Planets follow Keplerian orbits governed by gravitational interactions with the host star and each other
- **Formation from protoplanetary disks**: Systems form from rotating disks of gas and dust around young stars via accretion and gravitational collapse
- **Architectural diversity**: Discovered systems range from hot Jupiters (gas giants in tight orbits) to multi-planet systems in compact resonant chains
- **Habitability constraints**: Planetary mass, composition, magnetic field, and orbital distance determine potential for liquid water and atmospheric retention
- **Tidal effects**: Close-in planets experience tidal locking, tidal heating, and orbital circularization from stellar gravitational interactions
