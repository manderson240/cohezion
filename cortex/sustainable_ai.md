# Sustainable AI

The practice of designing, training, and deploying AI systems with awareness of their environmental, economic, and social costs. Sustainable AI addresses the growing energy and carbon footprint of large-scale model training and inference, advocating for carbon-aware scheduling, compute-efficient architectures, and transparent reporting of environmental impact alongside performance metrics.

## Definition

Sustainable AI encompasses the full lifecycle environmental impact of machine learning systems: from the embodied carbon in hardware manufacturing, through the electricity consumed during training and inference, to the electronic waste generated by hardware refresh cycles. The field draws on Green AI principles (Schwartz et al., 2020), which argue that the research community's emphasis on accuracy gains has led to exponentially growing compute requirements, and proposes efficiency as a first-class evaluation criterion alongside accuracy.

## Key Properties

- **Carbon footprint awareness** -- Training a single large language model can emit hundreds of tonnes of CO2-equivalent, comparable to multiple transatlantic flights or the lifetime emissions of several cars
- **Compute-optimal training** -- Scaling laws (Chinchilla, Kaplan) demonstrate that training smaller models on more data is more compute-efficient than training larger models on less data
- **Carbon-aware scheduling** -- Shifting training jobs to times and locations with cleaner electricity grids reduces carbon intensity without changing compute requirements
- **Reuse over retraining** -- Transfer learning, fine-tuning, and model distillation amortize the environmental cost of large model training across many downstream applications
- **Reporting standards** -- Proposals for mandatory reporting of training energy, hardware utilization, and carbon emissions alongside model performance metrics

## Examples

- **Green AI metric** -- Schwartz et al. proposed reporting FLOPs alongside accuracy to make computational cost visible in research papers
- **Carbon-aware Azure scheduling** -- Microsoft implemented carbon-aware workload scheduling that shifts batch training to periods of high renewable energy availability
- **Model distillation chains** -- Large teacher models trained once, then distilled into many smaller student models, amortizing the training carbon cost

Cohezion's token efficiency patterns are a direct application of sustainable AI principles at the agent orchestration layer. By minimizing redundant LLM calls through knowledge reuse, concept caching, and context management, the framework reduces the per-session compute and energy cost of AI-assisted development. The vault's persistent memory system amortizes research effort across sessions, embodying the reuse-over-recompute principle that sustainable AI advocates.
