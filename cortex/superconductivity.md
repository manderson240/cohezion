# Superconductivity

Superconductivity is the phenomenon where certain materials exhibit exactly zero electrical resistance and expel magnetic fields (the Meissner effect) below a characteristic critical temperature. Discovered in 1911 by Kamerlingh Onnes in mercury at 4.2 K, superconductivity remains one of the most dramatic manifestations of quantum mechanics at macroscopic scales.

## Definition

A superconductor is a material that, below its critical temperature (Tc), critical magnetic field (Hc), and critical current density (Jc), conducts electricity with zero resistance and exhibits perfect diamagnetism. The transition to the superconducting state is a quantum phase transition involving the formation of a macroscopic quantum condensate.

## Key Concepts

### BCS Theory (Conventional Superconductors)

Proposed by Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer in 1957 (Nobel Prize 1972), BCS theory explains superconductivity as the condensation of Cooper pairs — pairs of electrons bound by phonon-mediated attractive interactions. Key predictions:

- **Cooper pairs:** Electrons with opposite spin and momentum pair via lattice vibration (phonon) exchange, forming boson-like quasiparticles
- **Energy gap:** A gap opens in the electronic density of states, preventing scattering and enabling zero resistance
- **Meissner effect:** The condensate's macroscopic wavefunction generates screening currents that expel magnetic flux
- **Isotope effect:** Tc depends on the isotopic mass of lattice atoms, confirming phonon involvement

Paradoxically, the best normal conductors (copper, silver, gold) are not superconductors because their weak electron-phonon coupling prevents Cooper pair formation.

### High-Temperature Superconductors

Discovered in 1986 by Bednorz and Muller (Nobel Prize 1987), cuprate superconductors achieve Tc up to 135 K — far above the BCS limit. The pairing mechanism involves magnetic spin fluctuations rather than phonons. Recent experiments have directly visualized superconductivity at the atomic scale in these crystals, providing strong evidence for the spin-fluctuation mechanism.

### Room-Temperature Superconductivity

The quest for ambient-condition superconductivity continues. In 2025, the zentropy theory framework connected BCS theory with density functional theory (DFT), enabling computational prediction of superconducting behavior in both conventional and unconventional materials. This approach models the atomic lattice as a flexible structure ("pontoon bridge") that maintains electron superhighways even under thermal vibrations.

## Applications

| Application | How It Uses Superconductivity |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| MRI machines | Superconducting magnets produce strong, stable magnetic fields |
| Particle accelerators | Superconducting RF cavities and dipole magnets (LHC uses 1,232 dipoles) |
| Quantum computing | Superconducting transmon qubits (used by IBM, Google) |
| Power transmission | High-Tc cables for lossless electricity delivery |
| Maglev trains | Superconducting magnets for magnetic levitation |
| SQUID sensors | Superconducting quantum interference devices for ultra-sensitive magnetometry |
