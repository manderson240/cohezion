## Definition

Massive evolved stars (10-40 solar masses initially) with temperatures of 3,500-4,500 K and radii up to 1,000 solar radii, representing the late evolutionary phase before core collapse. They exist for only 300,000-1,000,000 years before supernova. The prototypical red supergiant Betelgeuse underwent the 2019-2020 'Great Dimming' when a convective updraft ejected superhot plasma creating an obscuring dust cloud.

## Key Properties

- Largest stars by volume (up to 1,000 solar radii) with intermediate surface temperatures
- Final burning stage before core collapse, fusing helium and heavier elements
- Catastrophic mass-loss rates through stellar winds (~1 solar mass per 10,000 years)
- Lifespans of only 300,000-1,000,000 years as red supergiants before Type II supernovae
- Dynamic surface convection with giant cells that eject massive plasma outbursts

## Examples

- Betelgeuse (~700 solar radii): Great Dimming of 2019-2020 from convective plasma ejection condensing into obscuring dust
- Antares A: comparable first-magnitude red supergiant in Scorpius
