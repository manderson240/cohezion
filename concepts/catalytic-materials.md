## Definition

Engineered materials designed to accelerate specific chemical reactions while remaining unconsumed, including superatom clusters that outperform single-atom catalysts. Kilic et al. (2024) demonstrated superatomic clusters like Li3O activate CO2 far more effectively than individual atoms, stretching C=O bonds from 1.16 A to 1.30 A.

## Key Properties

- Superatomic clusters (Li3O, ZrO, Cu45) mimic individual atom chemistry with superior molecular activation
- CO2 activation dramatically enhanced: near-zero free energy change on graphene substrate
- Tunable magnetic properties enable control of spin effects in catalytic mechanisms
- Single-superatom catalysts outperform precious metal catalysts while being more stable and cost-effective
- Applications span hydrogen production, CO2 conversion, and energy storage

## Examples

- Li3O superatom on graphene: achieves superior CO2 binding enabling direct carbon capture and fuel conversion
- ZrO superatom (2025): matches or exceeds Pd performance in Suzuki reactions for pharmaceutical synthesis
